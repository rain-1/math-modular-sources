6. Which primes?

The best prime is usually not arbitrary.

For Catalan, p=2 is exceptional because the half-integral parameters and conductor 4 generate very large powers of 2. That is why the slope 24 appeared.

For other motives, one should search primes where:

the hypergeometric parameters have large p-adic denominator effects;

the conductor has strong p-part;

Frobenius has special slope structure;

there is ordinary/unit-root geometry;

two realizations have different p-adic convergence slopes.

Small primes are particularly attractive. Calegari's modular method, for example, succeeds for ζp​(3) at p=2,3, while the same simple criterion does not automatically work at all other genus-zero primes. University of Chicago Math Department

But for our method, an odd prime need not by itself prove any p-adic irrationality. It can still be valuable simply by contributing divisibility to the cross determinant.

That distinction is important:

a prime can be useful as an arithmetic bridge even when its p-adic period is not the final target.​
So I would search all relevant primes, calculate their cross-determinant slopes, and sum their adelic capacities.

The most immediate research task, however, is clear: prove the primitive-denominator sieve for the Catalan lattice and test k=23.
 If that works, the current construction may already contain enough 
arithmetic to cross the irrationality threshold without discovering a 
new approximation family.
