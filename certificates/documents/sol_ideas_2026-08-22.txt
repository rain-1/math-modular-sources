Yes. There are other known cases, but the genuinely difficult ones are remarkably rare.

The biggest recent example is
[
\boxed{L(2,\chi_{-3})\ \text{is irrational}.}
]
Calegari–Dimitrov–Tang proved this in 2024, and in fact proved the stronger statement
[
\boxed{1,\ \zeta(2),\ L(2,\chi_{-3})\ \text{are }\mathbf Q\text{-linearly independent}.}
]
They emphasize how exceptional this is: apart from the “easy parity” Dirichlet values that are algebraic multiples of powers of (\pi), essentially the only previous explicit Dirichlet (L)-value of this type known irrational was (\zeta(3)). ([arXiv][1])

What is particularly relevant to us is that their proof uses an arithmetic-holonomy refinement of Apéry-limit constructions. In other words, it is philosophically very close to what we have been doing: exploit the differential equation, analytic continuation past the first singularity, and unusually good denominator arithmetic, rather than merely finding a fast decimal approximation. 

There is now a very clean criterion visible from our (\Psi) example. Suppose
[
A(t)=\sum a_nt^n,\qquad
B(t)=\sum b_nt^n,
\qquad
\frac{b_n}{a_n}\to\lambda,
]
and
[
P(t)=B(t)-\lambda A(t)
]
continues through the first singularity and has radius (R). If a common denominator (D_n) satisfies
[
D_na_n,\ D_nb_n\in\mathbb Z
]
and
[
\boxed{\limsup_{n\to\infty}D_n^{1/n}<R,}
]
then
[
D_n(b_n-\lambda a_n)\to0,
]
which gives the Apéry irrationality contradiction if (\lambda\in\mathbb Q).

For our square-root Apéry form we have just found
[
D_n=
2^{2n-s_2(n)-\lfloor\log_2n\rfloor-1}d_n^2.
]
Hence
[
\lim D_n^{1/n}=4e^2.
]
But the companion combination survives analytically all the way to
[
R=(1+\sqrt2)^4.
]
And crucially
[
\boxed{4e^2<(1+\sqrt2)^4.}
]

That one inequality is essentially the arithmetic/analytic “worthiness test” for the irrationality proof. It explains exactly why this example works.

This gives us a way to screen other modular (L)-values.

The first target I would investigate seriously is
[
\boxed{L(3,\chi_5),\qquad \chi_5(n)=\left(\frac n5\right).}
]
This is a genuinely difficult, opposite-parity Dirichlet value. Beukers already got extremely close: his 1987 modular construction proves
[
\boxed{
8\zeta(3)-5\sqrt5,L(3,\chi_5)
\notin\mathbb Q(\sqrt5).
}
]
He does this with a weight-4 modular form on (\Gamma_1(5)), a Fricke transformation, algebraic conjugate recurrences, and denominator estimates. ([Numdam][2])

But this does not prove (L(3,\chi_5)) itself irrational.

That is exactly why I think it is now unusually attractive. Beukers has already supplied most of the geometry; what we would need to do is separate the two periods (\zeta(3)) and (L(3,\chi_5)), perhaps by the sort of refined holonomy/multiple-monodromy argument that Calegari–Dimitrov–Tang used to turn mixed-period information into the pure (L(2,\chi_{-3})) theorem. CDT explicitly discuss Beukers' (\chi_5) mixed-period result as an antecedent of their work. ([Department of Mathematics][3])

The second obvious target is Catalan:
[
\boxed{G=L(2,\chi_{-4}).}
]
It remains individually open. The strongest finite-set result I found is that at least one of
[
\beta(2),\beta(4),\beta(6),\beta(8),\beta(10)
]
is irrational. ([arXiv][4])

There is an important warning here. CDT explicitly analyze two well-known Apéry-type approximation families for Catalan and show that their analytic/denominator constants miss the necessary inequality—by a wide margin. So merely having a beautiful recurrence and a harmonic companion is not enough.  This agrees rather strikingly with what we discovered independently in our Catalan work: one needs a different realization with a better analytic branch-unfolding/denominator ratio.

There are also many modular (L(f,2)) values sitting ready-made in Yifan Yang's Apéry-limit paper. For example
[
\boxed{
f(\tau)=\eta(\tau)^3\eta(7\tau)^3
}
]
is a weight-3 cusp form, and Yang constructs rational sequences with
[
\frac{b_n}{a_n}\longrightarrow L(f,2).
]
The corresponding Hauptmodul has critical values
[
0,\quad\frac1{28},\quad1,\quad\infty,
]
and the special linear combination (B-L(f,2)A) is analytic through (1/28). ([arXiv][5])

Unfortunately, this already tells us why the elementary Apéry criterion fails: after killing (1/28), the next singularity is only at
[
R=1.
]
There is no room whatsoever for exponentially growing denominator clearing.

Likewise Yang studies
[
f(\tau)=\eta(2\tau)^3\eta(6\tau)^3,
]
with singularities
[
0,\quad-\frac19,\quad-\frac18,\quad\infty.
]
The (L(f,2))-combination removes the (-1/9) singularity, but the next one at (-1/8) is far too close. ([arXiv][5])

So these are beautiful (L)-value Apéry limits, but not immediate irrationality proofs.

There is another large reservoir worth testing: the six Zagier sporadic sequences. Osburn–Straub proved that their interpolations are critical (L)-values of weight-3 modular forms. ([arXiv][6]) They also show, more generally, an infinite family of cellular sequences evaluating to critical modular (L)-values. For example the original interpolated Apéry numbers satisfy
[
A!\left(-\frac12\right)
=\frac{16}{\pi^2}
L!\left(\eta(2\tau)^4\eta(4\tau)^4,2\right).
]
([arXiv][7])

Those six sporadics are especially appealing now because we have precisely the machinery needed to ask the right question for each one:

[
\boxed{
\text{denominator entropy}
\quad\text{versus}\quad
\text{post-cancellation analytic radius}.
}
]

That is much sharper than asking merely “does it have an Apéry recurrence?”

There are also general existence theorems. Fischler proved that, for a fixed Dirichlet character, many sufficiently high integer (L)-values of the appropriate parity are irrational or even linearly independent. But those theorems generally do not tell you which particular small value is irrational. ([arXiv][8]) Similarly, infinitely many odd zeta values are irrational, while results such as “at least two of (\zeta(5),\zeta(7),\ldots,\zeta(35)) are irrational” do not currently isolate (\zeta(5)) or (\zeta(7)). ([arXiv][4])

So my ranking for genuinely interesting individual targets would be:

1. (\mathbf{L(3,\chi_5)}) — strongest immediate candidate. Beukers already built almost exactly the required modular machine, but obtained only a mixed-period theorem.
2. The six weight-3 modular (L(f,2))'s attached to Zagier's sporadic sequences — finite census, excellent arithmetic, and likely amenable to our exact denominator/branch-radius test.
3. (G=L(2,\chi_{-4})) — highest-value target, but the known approximation geometries provably fail the straightforward criterion.
4. Other opposite-parity quadratic (L(2,\chi_D)) and (L(3,\chi_D)) values — now much more plausible after CDT, but likely require genuine arithmetic holonomy rather than plain Apéry convergence.

And there is a striking contemporary confirmation that this is the right screening philosophy: Bortolotto–Oliveira's May 2026 paper revisits Beukers' modular proof, finds a whole affine family of level-6 constructions that still prove (\zeta(3)) irrational, then tests several other genus-zero Fricke groups. The obstruction in the unsuccessful cases is precisely the failure to get the necessary combination of analytic decay and denominator growth. ([arXiv][9])

So yes: I think there is a real research project here. The next thing I would do is an exact **irrationality census of the six Zagier sporadics plus Beukers' level-5 (\chi_5) system**, computing for each
[
(R,; \lim D_n^{1/n},; R/\lim D_n^{1/n}),
]
and then attack any case with ratio (>1). That would tell us very quickly whether another Beukers-style irrational modular (L)-value is hiding in plain sight.
