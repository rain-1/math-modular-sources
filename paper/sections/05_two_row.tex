\section{Two-row constructions: the master formula and the design rule}
\label{sec:tworow}

A single Ap\'ery row proves irrationality when its linear form decays faster
than its denominators grow.  When no single row does, one may try to combine
two rows carrying the \emph{same} period.  This section states the closed-form
quality of such a construction (Theorem~E), derives from it a design rule that
decides in three numbers whether a candidate pair can work, and applies it to
the four cases where the arithmetic has been computed.  Nothing here is an
irrationality theorem; the calibration values are all $<1$, and \S\ref{sec:5.5}
explains structurally why.

\subsection{The correlated congruence lattice}\label{sec:5.1}

We recall the construction of the Catalan papers \cite{ProjCatalan58,
ProjCatalanWindow} in the form used below.  Let $\alpha\in\R$ be the common
period.  Two integer rows are given,
\[
(X_{1,n},Y_{1,n}),\ (X_{2,n},Y_{2,n})\in\Z^2,\qquad
\lambda_{r,n}=X_{r,n}\alpha-Y_{r,n},
\]
obtained from the two Ap\'ery rows after sampling (row $1$ at index $\alpha n$,
row $2$ at $\gamma n$), clearing denominators by a common factor $S_n$ with
$\log S_n=Kn+o(n)$, and applying any extra integralizing multiplier.  Write
$A_r,E_r$ for the exponential rates of $X_{r,n}$ and $\lambda_{r,n}$.  The
mixed minor, after removing the common $S_n$ from the first coordinates
($\alpha_{r,n}=X_{r,n}/S_n$),
\[
h_n=\alpha_{1,n}Y_{2,n}-\alpha_{2,n}Y_{1,n},
\]
is the \emph{cross determinant}, and the arithmetic input of the method is a
\emph{proved} divisor $T_n\mid h_n$ with $\tfrac1n\log T_n\to G$.  Setting
$M_n=S_nT_n$, the \emph{correlated congruence lattice} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Kn}
K_n=\Bigl\{(c_1,c_2)\in\Z^2:\
\alpha_{1,n}c_1+\alpha_{2,n}c_2\equiv0\ (T_n),\quad
Y_{1,n}c_1+Y_{2,n}c_2\equiv0\ (M_n)\Bigr\},
\end{equation}
so that $q(c)=(c_1X_{1,n}+c_2X_{2,n})/M_n$ and
$p(c)=(c_1Y_{1,n}+c_2Y_{2,n})/M_n$ are integers.  A two-line Smith-normal-form
computation gives
\[
[\Z^2:K_n]
=\frac{S_nT_n^2}{\gcd(h_n,T_nY_{1,n},T_nY_{2,n},S_nT_n\alpha_{1,n},
S_nT_n\alpha_{2,n},S_nT_n^2)}\ \le\ S_nT_n=M_n
\]
because $T_n\mid h_n$; passing to a sublattice $L_n\subseteq K_n$ with
$M_n\le\operatorname{covol}(L_n)<2M_n$, the division modulus and the
coefficient-lattice covolume have the same exponential rate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigma}
\sigma=K+G .
\end{equation}
\emph{This is the whole point of the word ``correlated'': the determinant
divisibility is used in two congruences at the cost of one copy of $T_n$
rather than $T_n^2$.}

The selection step is the two-successive-minima theorem of
\cite[Thm.~5.1]{ProjCatalanWindow}, stated there for an arbitrary real
$\alpha$ and used here as a black box.

\begin{theorem}[two-minimum selection; {\cite[Thm.~5.1]{ProjCatalanWindow}}]
\label{thm:selection}
\Proved{} Suppose $S_n\mid X_{1,n},X_{2,n}$, the lattice and output rows are
as above with $d_n=[\Z^2:L_n]$, the six two-sided rate statements hold,
$s_n=\tfrac1n\log S_n\to K$ with $0\le\kappa_n=\tfrac1n\log d_n\le s_n$, the
\emph{crossed gap} $A_2+E_1>A_1+E_2$ holds, and $H_n,F_n$ defined by
\[
x_n=\frac{\kappa_n+E_2-E_1}{2},\qquad
H_n=\kappa_n-x_n+A_2-s_n,\qquad
F_n=x_n+E_1-s_n
\]
are eventually bounded away from zero.  Then for all large $n$ one can select
$c_n\in L_n$ with $q_n=q(c_n)\neq0$, $\ell_n=q_n\alpha-p_n\neq0$,
\[
\log|q_n|\ge H_nn-o(n),\qquad
\frac{\log|\ell_n|}{\log|q_n|}\le\frac{F_n}{H_n}+o(1),
\]
hence worthiness $\delta\ge1-F_n/H_n$.
\end{theorem}

The proof applies Minkowski's second theorem to the anisotropic rectangle
$|u|\le e^{x_nn}$, $|v|\le e^{(\kappa_n-x_n)n}$ (area $4d_n$), takes vectors
attaining the two successive minima, and splits into three cases according to
the size of $r_n=-\tfrac1n\log\mu_{1,n}$ and whether the first minimum's linear
form vanishes.  The use of two minima rather than Minkowski's first theorem
alone is what prevents an exceptionally short \emph{rationality-kernel vector}
from spoiling the lower bound on $|q_n|$.

\begin{remark}[the hypothesis $F>0$ is not decorative]\label{rem:Fpos}
Hypothesis (4) of Theorem~\ref{thm:selection} --- $H_n$ and $F_n$ eventually
bounded below by a \emph{positive} constant --- is exactly what fails if one
pushes $\sigma$ past $E_1+E_2$.  In that regime $F_n<0$ and the method
degenerates: the box area equals the covolume, so Minkowski's first theorem in
that box \emph{is} Dirichlet's theorem, which produces such pairs for every
real number, rational ones included.  The proof's non-vanishing $\ell_n\neq0$
comes from the output-determinant identity, which says only that the two
minima's forms are not \emph{both} zero; when the first minimum has $\ell=0$
--- precisely the rationality-kernel case --- the argument switches to the
second vector, which is nonzero but carries no $e^{F_nn}$ upper bound.  The
construction can deliver ``$\ell_n\neq0$'' or ``$|\ell_n|\le e^{F_nn}$'', never
both.  This was verified by an explicit control experiment: replacing the
period $G$ by a rational $G^*=\operatorname{bestappr}(G,10^{320})$ leaves the
lattice \eqref{eq:Kn} unchanged (it never uses $G$) and reproduces the same
numerics, $|q_nG-p_n|=e^{-78.051}$ against $|q_nG^*-p_n|=e^{-78.050}$ at
$n=98$, with no contradiction because $1/\operatorname{den}(G^*)=e^{-735.7}$
\cite[\S4]{ProjCatalanAudit}.  Everything below is therefore stated in the
regime $F>0$, where the conclusion is a worthiness statement and nothing more.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Theorem E: the master formula}\label{sec:5.2}

Doing the bookkeeping of \S\ref{sec:5.1} in closed form gives one formula that
covers every instance computed in this project.

\begin{namedthm}[master formula]\label{thm:E}
\Proved{} modulo Theorem~\ref{thm:selection}.  Consider a two-row construction
in which
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
\item the \emph{decayer} is sampled at $\gamma n$, with $|a_n|\sim
\Lambda_{\rm dec}^{\gamma n}$ and linear form $\sim\lambda^{\gamma n}$,
$\lambda<1$, and $p$-adic slope $\sigma_{\rm dec}$;
\item the \emph{engine} is sampled at $\alpha n$, with linear form
$\sim(\rho_2^{\rm eng})^{\alpha n}$ (which may grow) and slope
$\sigma_{\rm eng}$;
\item the common denominator clearing has rate $S=k\cdot\max(\alpha,\gamma)$,
and any extra integralizing multiplier has rate $\eta$ per unit of $n$;
\item the hidden divisor rate is
$G=\sum_p\min(\sigma_p^{\rm eng}\alpha,\ \sigma_p^{\rm dec}\gamma)\log p$.
\end{itemize}
Then, in the notation of Theorem~\ref{thm:selection},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:master}
\boxed{\
F=\tfrac12\Bigl[\underbrace{S+\eta+\alpha\log\rho_2^{\rm eng}}_{\rm COST}
-\underbrace{\bigl(\gamma\log\tfrac1\lambda+G\bigr)}_{\rm RESOURCE}\Bigr],
\qquad
H=F+\gamma\log\frac{\Lambda_{\rm dec}}{\lambda},
\qquad
\delta=1-\frac FH\ }
\end{equation}
and $H-F=A_{\rm dec}-E_{\rm dec}$.  In words: \emph{$F$ is exactly one half of
the cost--resource deficit.}
\end{namedthm}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from $x=\tfrac12(\sigma+E_2-E_1)$, $H=A_2-x$, $F=x+E_1-\sigma$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:selection} with $\sigma=K+G$ as in \eqref{eq:sigma}, after
substituting the four rates
$A_{\rm eng}=S+\eta+\alpha\log\Lambda_{\rm eng}$,
$E_{\rm eng}=S+\eta+\alpha\log\rho_2^{\rm eng}$,
$A_{\rm dec}=S+\gamma\log\Lambda_{\rm dec}$,
$E_{\rm dec}=S+\gamma\log\lambda$, and simplifying.  (The two-row roles are
labelled so that the decayer plays the part of row $2$ in
Theorem~\ref{thm:selection}.)  The multi-prime form of $G$ is legitimate
because the correlated-lattice step of \S\ref{sec:5.1} needs only
$T_n\mid h_n$ and nothing about $T_n$ being a prime power; the Smith-normal-form
index bound goes through verbatim for $T_n=\prod_pp^{g_pn}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The design rule}\label{sec:designrule}

Formula \eqref{eq:master} has three inputs one can shop for.  Specializing it
turns the search for a two-row pair into a one-line test.

Suppose first that both rows are \emph{integral}, so $\eta=0$ and
$\kappa_p=0$ below, and take the balance point
$\alpha\sigma_{\rm eng}=\gamma\sigma_{\rm dec}$ at which the two $p$-adic
tails have equal slope (so that the minimum in $G$ is attained twice).  Then
for a single slope prime $p$ with $\log|c|=\sigma_p\log p$, the product formula
$\Lambda\lambda=c$ of \S\ref{sec:padic} gives
$\gamma\log\tfrac1\lambda+G=\gamma\log\Lambda_{\rm dec}$ \emph{exactly}, and
\eqref{eq:master} collapses to

\begin{proposition}[design rule, integral rows]\label{prop:design}
\Proved{} With the above hypotheses,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:designrule}
\boxed{\ \delta>1
\iff \log\Lambda_{\rm dec}>k\max(r,1)+r\log\rho_2^{\rm eng},
\qquad r=\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}=\frac{\sigma_{\rm dec}}{\sigma_{\rm eng}} .\ }
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

What the decaying row contributes is therefore its \emph{numerator growth}
$\log\Lambda$, not its archimedean decay: the $p$-adic slope makes up the whole
difference, automatically.  The method is a machine for converting the product
formula into Diophantine decay.  The quantity $\log\Lambda-k$ is the
\emph{budget} of a row.

Two corrections must be attached to \eqref{eq:designrule}, and both matter.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(i) Order $>2$.}  The step
$\gamma\log\tfrac1\lambda+G=\gamma\log\Lambda$ uses
$\Lambda\lambda=c$, an order-two identity.  For a scalar recurrence of order
$m$ with roots $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_m$ the product formula reads
$\sum_p\sigma_p\log p=\log|\prod_i\lambda_i|$, so the correct row score, valid
at any order, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orderm}
\text{score}=\log\frac1{|\lambda_2|}-k,\qquad
\text{harvestable budget}=\text{score}+\log\Bigl|\prod_i\lambda_i\Bigr|,
\end{equation}
which reduces to $\log|\lambda_1|-k$ only when $m=2$.  Using $\log\lambda_1-k$
on a higher-order row overstates the budget, sometimes wildly: the level-$12$
$\zeta(5)$ row has harvestable budget $-5$, not $+5.54$.  All the rows used
below are of order two, so \S\S\ref{sec:5.1}--\ref{sec:worked} are unaffected.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(ii) Non-integral rows and $\kappa$.}  Proposition~\ref{prop:design}
assumed $v_p(a_n)\ge0$.  If instead the row carries $p$-power denominators at
rate $\kappa_p$, i.e.\ $v_p(a_n)=-\kappa_pn+O(\log n)$, then the two
$-v_p(a)$ terms in the Casoratian slope law each contribute $+\kappa_pn$, and
by Proposition~\ref{prop:C} the slope is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kappaslope}
\sigma_p=v_p(c)+2\kappa_p .
\end{equation}
Clearing the denominators costs $\eta=\kappa_p\log p$ per index, so the
\emph{net} resource is $(\sigma_p-\kappa_p)\log p=(v_p(c)+\kappa_p)\log p$ and
the corrected budget is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kappabudget}
\boxed{\ \text{budget}=\log\Lambda-k+\sum_p\kappa_p\log p .\ }
\end{equation}
The $\kappa$ term is a second, independent source of $p$-adic resource,
\emph{invisible to the product formula}.  An integral row is capped at
$\log\Lambda-k$ as \eqref{eq:designrule} says; a non-integral row is not capped
there at all.  The measurement that forces this: Zudilin's Catalan row has
$c=\lambda_1\lambda_2=1$, so $v_2(c)=0$, yet its measured $2$-adic convergence
slope is $8$; and indeed $v_2(Q_m)=-4m+2s_2(m)$ exactly
\VerifiedR{$1\le m\le160$, zero exceptions}, so $\kappa_2=4$ and
$\sigma_2=0+2\cdot4=8$.  For comparison \eqref{eq:kappaslope} gives Domb
$6+0=6$, T $4+0=4$, Catalan's $\mathbf E$ row $5+0=5$, all matching.  With
$\log\Lambda=5\log\varphi$, $\kappa_2\log2=4\log2$ and $k=4$, Zudilin's row has
budget $+1.17864$, by far the largest of any row known --- and every unit of
its lead comes from $\kappa_2$.

\begin{remark}[multi-prime bridging]\label{rem:multiprime}
The bridge generalizes to several primes for free, with
$G=\sum_p\min(\sigma_p^{\rm eng}\alpha,\sigma_p^{\rm dec}\gamma)\log p$
(proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:E}).  But it does not raise the ceiling for integral
rows: since $\sum_p\sigma_p^{\rm dec}\log p=\log|c^{\rm dec}|$ and
$\log\tfrac1\lambda+\log|c|=\log\Lambda$, the resource is still at most
$\gamma\log\Lambda_{\rm dec}$; and the balance ratio $\alpha:\gamma$ is a
single number, so at most one prime can be balanced, the others contributing
their unbalanced minimum.  Moreover it is \emph{unrealizable in the census}:
across the twelve modular sporadic families the values of $c$ are
$-8,27,9,-1,32,72,81,-27,64,125,16,1$ for
$\mathbf A,\dots,\mathbf F,\delta,\zeta,\alpha,\eta,\varepsilon,\gamma$, so
$\mathbf F$ ($c=72=2^3\cdot3^2$) is the unique row with two slope primes.  Its
period $\tfrac58L(2,\chim3)$ is shared with $\mathbf B,\mathbf C,s_{18}$, all
of which have $\sigma_2=0$; since the per-prime term is a minimum over the two
rows, the $p=2$ contribution vanishes for every pair containing $\mathbf F$.
Multi-prime bridging is thus available in principle and realizable nowhere in
the canonical classification --- a clean negative, not an oversight.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection*{(a) Catalan, two versions}

The rows are Zudilin's Ap\'ery-like Catalan recurrence \cite{Zudilin2002a,
Zudilin2002b} --- $Q_0=1$, $Q_1=\tfrac74$, $P_0=0$, $P_1=\tfrac{13}8$, with
$\tfrac1m\log|Q_m|\to5\log\varphi$, $\tfrac1m\log|Q_mG-P_m|\to-5\log\varphi$,
$2^{e_m}Q_m\in\Z$, $2^{e_m}D_{2m-1}^2P_m\in\Z$, $e_m=4m+O(\log m)$ --- and the
modular $\mathbf E$ row of \S\ref{sec:sources}, with
$t=\eta_1^4\eta_4^2\eta_8^4/\eta_2^{10}$,
$F=\eta_2^{10}/(\eta_1^4\eta_4^4)$, source
$\Phi_{\mathbf E}=S-8S(2\tau)$ where $S=E_{3,\chim4,1}$, so
$L(\Phi_{\mathbf E},s)=(1-8\cdot2^{-s})\beta(s)\zeta(s-2)$ and
$L(\Phi_{\mathbf E},2)=G/2$; characteristic roots $8$ and $4$, $A_n\in\Z$,
$D_n^2B_n\in\Z$.  The arithmetic bridge is the statement that the two rows
converge to the \emph{same} $2$-adic Catalan period:

\begin{lemma}[common $2$-adic period; {\cite[Lem.~1.1]{ProjCatalan58}}]
\label{lem:G2}
There is $G_2\in\Q_2$ with
\[
v_2\Bigl(G_2-\frac{P_m}{Q_m}\Bigr)=8m-1-4s_2(m),\qquad
v_2\Bigl(G_2-\frac{2B_n}{A_n}\Bigr)=5n-1-4s_2(n),
\]
whence $v_2(P_mA_{2m}-2B_{2m}Q_m)=4m-1$, and for
$\Delta_{i,k}=P_iA_k-2Q_iB_k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:roof}
v_2(\Delta_{i,k})\ge\min\bigl\{4i-1-2s_2(i)+2s_2(k),\
5k-4i-1+2s_2(i)-2s_2(k)\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

The proof combines the two exact Wronskians with
$v_2(Q_m)=-4m+2s_2(m)$ and $v_2(A_n)=2s_2(n)$; the identification of the two
limits with $\zeta_2(2)$ uses Rivoal's moving Pad\'e specialization of Beukers'
continued fraction on the Zudilin side \cite{Rivoal2006} and the level-$8$ form
of Calegari's overconvergent $2$-adic construction on the modular side
\cite{Calegari2005}.  \todo{\cite{ProjCatalan58} labels this step ``Idea of
proof'', and \cite{ProjConsolidation} records the identification of the two
$2$-adic limits as the one genuine gap in the $0.9025$ paper.  It should be
cited as \Proved{} only after the moving-point passage is written out; until
then Lemma~\ref{lem:G2} is the hypothesis under which
Theorem~\ref{thm:E} is applied to this pair.}

The two affine slopes in \eqref{eq:roof} balance when $8i=5k$, which is the
arithmetic origin of the sampling ratio $i=5n$, $k=8n$; and since
$s_2(8n)=s_2(n)$, \eqref{eq:roof} gives
$v_2(\Delta_{5n,8n})\ge20n-O(\log n)$.  With $e_{5n}=20n+O(\log n)$ the total
divisor rate is $G=40\log2$.  Feeding
$\gamma=5$, $\alpha=8$, $k=2$, $S=2\max(10,8)=20$, $\eta=\kappa_2\gamma\log2
=20\log2$, $\rho_2^{\rm eng}=4$, $\lambda=\varphi^{-5}$,
$\Lambda_{\rm dec}=\varphi^5$ into \eqref{eq:master}:
\[
F=\tfrac12\bigl[20+20\log2+16\log2-25\log\varphi-40\log2\bigr]
=10-2\log2-\tfrac{25}2\log\varphi,\qquad
H=F+50\log\varphi=10-2\log2+\tfrac{75}2\log\varphi,
\]
so $\delta=(H-F)/H=50\log\varphi/(10-2\log2+\tfrac{75}2\log\varphi)
=0.902526602856971\ldots$, reproducing \cite{ProjCatalan58} exactly.  The same
paper shows that $r=c/a=8/5$ is the unique optimum inside this scalar
architecture: with $H(a,c)=\max(2a,c)+(2a+c)\log2-\tfrac12\min(8a,5c)\log2
+\tfrac{15}2a\log\varphi$ and $\delta=10a\log\varphi/H$, $H(r)$ is strictly
decreasing for $0<r<8/5$ and strictly increasing beyond.

At the coarser sampling $3n:6n$ the same formula gives

$\alpha\log\rho_2^{\rm eng}=12\log2$,
$G=\min(5\cdot6,8\cdot3)\log2=24\log2$, hence
$F=6-\tfrac{15}2\log\varphi$, $H=6+\tfrac{45}2\log\varphi$, and
\[
\delta_{ZE}=\frac{30\log\varphi}{6+\tfrac{45}2\log\varphi}
=0.8579144524736\ldots
\]

Against the Nesterenko $(4,7)$ row instead of $\mathbf E$, with no bridge at

$F=\tfrac{E_Z+E_N-12}2=7.746367$, $H=F+(A_N-E_N)=22.707996$,

best-reduced-basis vector rather than the prescribed anisotropic box
\cite[\S15]{ProjZeta3}.  Adding the missing second congruence to the same rows
raises the measured $\delta$ from $0.646$ to $\approx1.01$, converging to $1$
from above --- but $\log|q_nG-p_n|$ stays between $-13$ and $-23$ for all $n$
tested and does \emph{not} tend to $-\infty$.  That is worthiness $1$, the
knife-edge $F=0$; it is not irrationality, and it agrees exactly with the
formalization.

The Casoratian $a_nb_{n-1}-a_{n-1}b_n=-c^{\,n-1}/n^3$ gives exactly

=:\iota(n)$ \Proved{}, so $b_n/a_n$ is $2$-adically Cauchy with

the minimum.

everything else fixed, $\delta\ge1$ iff
$k\le k^*=\log(12+8\sqrt2)-\tfrac43\log2=2.2248452944$, whereas $k=3$ is sharp:

$\mu(\zeta(3))\le31.4$, worse than Ap\'ery's $13.42$ and far from Rhin--Viola;
the only possible value of this architecture would be as a new proof, never as
a measure.

\subsubsection*{(c) $L(2,\chim3)$: four aligned rows and no decayer}

\subsection{Why cross-world pairs}\label{sec:5.5}

Observation~\ref{obs:decayer} has a structural explanation, and it is
\eqref{eq:kappaslope}.

\begin{proposition}[cross-world principle]\label{prop:crossworld}
\Proved{} from Proposition~\ref{prop:C}.  A modular Ap\'ery row of the kind
classified in \S\ref{sec:sources} has $a_n\in\Z$ automatically (triangular
peeling against $t\in q\Z[[q]]$), hence $\kappa_p=0$ at every $p$, hence
$\sigma_p=v_p(c)$ and budget $\le\log\Lambda-k$.  A hypergeometric row
--- Beukers/Rivoal Pad\'e, Rhin--Viola integrals, Zudilin's and Nesterenko's
constructions --- acquires $\kappa_p>0$ from its factorial/binomial
normalization, and is not capped there.  Therefore ``you need a cross-world
partner'' is precisely ``you need a partner with $\kappa_p>0$, and only the
hypergeometric world supplies it''.
\end{proposition}

Three consequences.  First, the search criterion \emph{inverts}: one should not
look for rows with $p\mid c$, but for rows with large $p$-power
\emph{denominators}.  Second, the claim that ``no amount of cleverness can
exceed $\gamma\log\Lambda_{\rm dec}$'', which would have made the $\zeta(3)$
failure a statement about the $(12,4,16)$ motive, is true only for integral
rows and is retracted here: the $\zeta(3)$ failure is a fact about the two rows
we happen to have, both of which are integral --- indeed
$v_2(a^{\mathbf T}_n)\ge0$ is \emph{positive}, which slightly reduces the
slope.  Third, this yields sharp targets: a hypergeometric $\zeta(3)$ row with
$2$-power denominators (Rhin--Viola's $\zeta(3)$ integrals being the obvious
place) paired with Domb as engine; and, for the weight-three cusp row of
\S\ref{sec:thmB} with its engine parameters
$k=2$, $\sigma_2=2$, $\log\rho_2^{\rm eng}=\log4$ (the recurrence
$(n+1)^2a_{n+1}=(20n^2+10n+2)a_n-16(2n-1)^2a_{n-1}$ is not in Zagier
normalisation: its trailing coefficient $64(n-\tfrac12)^2$ gives Casoratian
$\propto4^n\binom{2n}{n}^2$, hence slope $2$ rather than $v_2(64)=6$; see
Remark~\ref{rem:nonzagier}), a decaying partner with
period $L(f,2)$, $f=\eta(2\tau)^3\eta(6\tau)^3$, and
\[
\Lambda_{\rm dec}>14.8,\ 29.6,\ 160
\qquad\text{according as}\qquad
\sigma_{\rm dec}=1,\ 2,\ 3 ,
\]
by \eqref{eq:designrule}.  The exhaustive eta-quotient census offers evidence
against finding it inside the modular world: over all fourteen genus-zero
levels and $520\,005$ pairs, the largest characteristic root attached to a
known-period Ap\'ery-like row is Ap\'ery's own $\Lambda=12+8\sqrt2+\dots=33.97$,
and raising the level does not raise $\log\Lambda$ --- levels $10,13,18$
contribute nothing, and levels $12,16$ only reproduce rows already present at
$4,6,8,9$ \cite{ProjSporadicSearch}.  Lowering $\log\rho_2^{\rm eng}$, by
contrast, is the cheapest lever in \eqref{eq:master} and has never been
optimized: every construction so far took whatever engine happened to be
aligned, rather than the one with the slowest-growing linear form.
