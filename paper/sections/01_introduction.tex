\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

An \emph{Ap\'ery system} for a real number $\xi$ is a pair of sequences $a_n\in\mathbb Z$, $b_n\in\mathbb Q$ satisfying one linear recurrence with polynomial coefficients, with
\[
a_n\asymp\lambda_1^n,\qquad |a_n\xi-b_n|\asymp\lambda_2^n,\qquad d_n^{\,k}b_n\in\mathbb Z,\qquad d_n=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\dots,n),
\]
and $\xi$ is irrational as soon as $\lambda_2e^k<1$. Ap\'ery's systems for $\zeta(2)$ and $\zeta(3)$ satisfy this with room to spare; in the forty years since, a large number of structurally similar systems have been found---Zagier's and Almkvist--Zudilin's sporadic families, Cooper's, the Ramanujan-Machine continued fractions, and many constructed from modular forms---and none of them does. This paper is about why.

Beukers' modular interpretation \cite{Beukers1987} supplies the three ingredients of such a system: a genus-zero group $\Gamma$, a Hauptmodul $t$ with integral $q$-expansion, and a modular form $f$ of weight $w$ with $\sum a_nt^n=f$. The companion $b$ is then an Eichler integral, and the special value $\xi$ is a period of the corresponding Eisenstein or cuspidal extension. Our starting point is that the three quantities in the inequality above are controlled by three \emph{separate} pieces of this data: the special value by the \emph{source} $\Phi=f\cdot Dt$ (Section~\ref{sec:sources}); the rates $\lambda_1,\lambda_2$ by the position of the cusps and elliptic points of $\Gamma$ in the coordinate $t$ (Section~\ref{sec:arch}); and the denominator depth $k$ by the number of integrations minus the number that are free. A fourth quantity, invisible in the inequality but decisive for what follows, is the $p$-adic behaviour of the ratios $b_n/a_n$ (Section~\ref{sec:padic}).

\subsection*{Results}

\emph{Sources.} For the twelve sporadic families with modular parametrisations the source $\Phi$ is a holomorphic Eisenstein series with zero cuspidal projection (Theorem~A, proved in the project papers cited there), and the Ap\'ery limit is a rational multiple of a critical value $L(\Phi,w+1)$ (Theorem~B). Eisenstein sources give Dirichlet $L$-values; cuspidal sources give critical values of cusp forms, of which we give two examples on the Domb curve, with limits $\tfrac12L(f_6,3)$ and $L(\eta(2\tau)^3\eta(6\tau)^3,2)$. Period annihilation---the cancellation of unwanted critical values by oldform combinations, of which Ap\'ery's $E_4-28E_4(2\tau)+63E_4(3\tau)-36E_4(6\tau)$ is the prototype---is finite Vandermonde algebra, and it produces new systems on demand (level-$16$ $\zeta(5)$, a level-$12$ $\zeta(7)$ parent with limit $\tfrac{209}{1728}\zeta(7)$, $\beta(4)$ at level $24$, $L(4,\chi_{-3})$).

\emph{Slopes.} For a three-term recurrence with constant term $c=\lambda_1\lambda_2$ the Casoratian is $-c^{n-1}/n^{w+1}$, so $b_n/a_n$ converges $p$-adically with slope $\sigma_p=v_p(c)+2\kappa_p$, where $\kappa_p$ is the rate of $p$-power denominators of $a_n$ (Proposition~C). For integral rows the product formula gives $\sum_p\sigma_p\log p=\log|\lambda_1\lambda_2|$: archimedean imperfection is exactly redistributed as $p$-adic convergence. Ap\'ery's rows ($c=\pm1$) have none; Zagier's Catalan row ($c=32$) has $\sigma_2=5$.

\emph{Rigidity.} Rows sharing a period and a slope prime have $p$-adic limits in the same rational ratio as their archimedean limits (Conjecture~D, verified in every available case, including one where the archimedean limit does not exist). This is the precise sense in which two realisations of one extension class are ``aligned'', and it is the mechanism behind the two-row constructions for Catalan's constant.

\emph{The two-row design rule.} For two aligned rows the quality of the correlated-lattice construction of \cite{ProjCatalanLCM,ProjCatalan58} has a closed form (Theorem~E) which reproduces every number in the literature ($0.8579$, $0.9025$, $0.6589$, and the formally verified worthiness $1-\varepsilon$ of \cite{CatalanLean}) and a new one: the Domb and $(12,4,16)$ rows for $\zeta(3)$ are aligned at $p=2$ and give $\delta=0.9010$. At the balance point the product formula collapses the formula to a design rule, $\delta>1\iff\log\Lambda_{\rm dec}>k\max(r,1)+r\log\rho_2^{\rm eng}$, which explains why exactly Catalan and $\zeta(3)$ come within ten percent of the threshold and every other known pair does not: the binding constraint is the existence of a \emph{decaying} partner, and modular rows, being integral, never supply one---only hypergeometric rows with $\kappa_p>0$ do.

\emph{Past the threshold.} The Catalan construction is supercritical: its $2$-adic divisibility exceeds the break-even rate. We show that nothing expressible through valuations---extra congruences, factorisation of the cross determinant, extra rows, directional divisibility of coefficients---can exclude the rationality-kernel vector in that regime, and that the only remaining statement, Lemma~P2 of Section~\ref{sec:threshold}, is of irrationality strength. This is a negative result with a positive reading: the method converts the irrationality of Catalan's constant, without loss, into a single statement about one explicit moment lattice, and we record what an attack on it must look like (Problem~\ref{prob:adelic-pade}).

\subsection*{What this paper does not claim}
No new irrationality theorem. Every displayed quality $\delta$ is either below $1$ or, where the formal expression exceeds $1$, accompanied by the control experiment showing that a rational number produces the same numbers. Evidence grades are attached to every statement: \Proved{} means a written proof or machine-checked certificate exists and is cited; \emph{verified} means an exact finite computation to a stated range; conjectures are labelled as such. The computations are reproducible from the scripts listed in Appendix~\ref{app:scripts}.
