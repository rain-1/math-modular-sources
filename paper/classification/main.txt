             INTEGRAL SECOND-ORDER APÉRY-LIKE RECURRENCES
                    AND RATIONAL ELLIPTIC SURFACES

                                         CLAUDE (FABLE), WITH RIVER


       Abstract. We study the integral solutions of three-term recurrences (n + 1)2 un+1 = P (n)un −
       Q(n)un−1 , deg P, deg Q ≤ 2, in two tiers. Theorem A is a conditional classification and is proved: if
       the Picard–Fuchs operator of such a row is that of an elliptic surface over P1t , then for the Zagier shape
       (an2 + an + b, cn2 ) all four singular fibres are semistable, deg J = 12 exactly — by Riemann–Hurwitz
       together with a twist-parity constraint 12 | deg J — and the configuration is, up to quadratic twist,
       one of Beauville’s six; no hypothesis on the Kodaira dimension is needed, since a twist raises χ
       (two branch points give a K3) without changing J , the local system, or the row. The row is the
       period expansion at a cusp in the integral coordinate, and the complete list of cusp placements is
       Zagier’s seven rows A–G, with A, C, F the three placements of I6 I3 I2 I1 and E, G the two of I1 I1 I2 I8 .
       The correspondence is Zagier’s (2009, §7) with Beauville’s theorem; new here are the mechanism
       — an exponent dictionary, Kodaira admissibility, a cross-ratio invariant, and a cusp-move theorem
       enumerating the placements — and the completeness of the list. We prove the analogue for the
       root-row shape (An2 + A    2
                                    n + B, C(2n − 1)2 ): there deg J = 6, three of Herfurtner’s configurations
       survive up to twist, and exactly two integral rows realise them. Riemann–Hurwitz alone does not
       force χ = 1 in either shape — in the root-row case a mixed III/III ∗ elliptic K3 is inside the bound
       — and it is the twist parity, not the degree bound, that settles it. Beukers’ row, the square root
       of Apéry’s ζ(3) recurrence, is not among them — it lives on X0 (6)/W6 , over which the universal
       curve does not descend. Theorem B collects the unconditional necessary conditions — Kodaira
       admissibility of the exponent differences, integrality rigidity (rational characteristic roots are integers,
       so the irrationality score is ≤ −k on every rational cusp-move orbit), and the Riemann–Hurwitz
       bound — which exclude the square roots of Cooper’s s10 , s18 and an infinite arctan-Padé family. The
       unconditional classification is exactly Zagier’s conjecture that any integral solution has a modular
       parametrisation, i.e. Bombieri–Dwork in rank two with four singular points; we make the reduction
       precise and give the evidence: exhaustive scans (1.27 × 106 integrality hits over 26 Kodaira-admissible

   Two further structural results are used throughout and stated in full below: the cusp-move theorem
(Theorem 6.1), which makes “which fibre sits at ∞” into a group action and supplies Theorem B(iii);
and the host-group theorem (Theorem 7.3), by which a three-term row forces its host to have exactly
four special points, hence covolume index ≤ 12, so that inside PSL2 (Z) there are exactly 28 candidate
groups, 16 congruence and 12 non-congruence — with Beauville’s six recovered as the four-cusp case.
Beukers’ Theorem 3 row is the unique positive-score row whose form is non-congruence.

The honest statement. Theorem A classifies the rows whose operator is assumed to be of geometric
origin. An unconditional classification — “every integral solution is on the list” — needs the converse,
and the converse is exactly Zagier’s conjecture: it is the rank-two, four-singular-point case of the
Bombieri–Dwork conjecture that a globally nilpotent operator comes from geometry. One direction of
the reduction is proved here: by Theorem 7.3 a modular parametrisation of a three-term row forces
four special points, hence one of 28 genus-zero groups, hence (with Theorems A and 7.4) the list. So
Zagier’s conjecture for this class is equivalent to the statement that the projective monodromy group of
an integral three-term row is commensurable with PSL2 (Z) (Proposition 5.6). That statement is not
accessible by rigidity — rank two with four singular points is not rigid in Katz’s sense, the accessory
parameter being the modulus — nor by the naive implication “globally nilpotent ⇒ hypergeometric
pullback”, for which Krammer’s Lamé example [13, 15] is the standing warning; the best partial
result we know is Beukers’ p-adic analysis of Dwork’s accessory parameter problem in exactly this
configuration [4], which does not close it. §5.8 gives the evidence in place of a proof. [OPEN]

                                                8. Outlook
K3 and higher, and five singular points. Proposition 4.2 and Lemma 4.3 close the K3 worry for
four singular points, but in a way worth restating precisely: an elliptic K3 over P1t with the row’s
                                                                                      ∗
P singular fibres is not excluded — in the root-row class the mixed III/III configuration with
four
   e = 24 is a genuine possibility — but every such surface is a quadratic twist of a rational one
with the same J , and deg J is pinned by the degree bound together with the parity, so the deg ≤ 12
search window was never the constraint and no new row can arise. What is not closed is a systematic
pass through Shimada–Zhang’s extremal elliptic K3s [18] against the 26 classes. [OPEN] Five singular
points means deg P or deg Q = 3, i.e. a four-term recurrence or cubic coefficients; there is no analogue
of Herfurtner’s theorem in that generality, and the cross-ratio becomes a two-parameter modulus, so
the finiteness argument of §3 does not survive. At the other end, Schmickler-Hirzebruch’s classification
of elliptic surfaces over P1 with three exceptional fibres [16] is the degenerate boundary: the case d = 0,
where t = ∞ merges with a finite singular point and the row becomes hypergeometric — Zagier’s
modular solutions #11 (16, 4, 0), #14 (27, 6, 0) and #20 (64, 12, 0) in the notation of Table 1.

A gate on the higher-rank constructions. For a linear-independence argument one wants a
Q-linear combination H of several Apéry limits whose linear form still decays — “fold-regularity”.
Lemma 8.1 (fold-regularity is codimension µ). [PROVED] Let λ1 be the dominant characteristic root,
of multiplicity µ. Near x1 = 1/λ1 the local solution space carries a Jordan block of size µ: solutions
y1 , y1 log, . . . , y1 logµ−1 up to holomorphic corrections. A solution has radius of convergence > |x1 | iff
all µ of its coordinates on that block vanish, whereas a hypothesised Q-linear relation among the limits
makes exactly the leading one vanish. Hence one relation implies fold-regularity iff µ = 1.
   For µ ≥ 2 the form does not decay at all — one gets polynomial convergence with |hn |1/n → |λ1 | —
so there is nothing for a holonomy bound, or for Beukers’ criterion, to act on. Verified sharply: a
20                                         CLAUDE (FABLE), WITH RIVER

row with µ = 1 folds and its form decays at rate λ2 exactly, while Sym3 of Zagier E (µ = 3) and a
Sym2 with µ = 2 do not fold at all. [VERIFIED: n up to 106 ] Symmetric powers and Cauchy squares
of a single row therefore raise µ and are the wrong place to look; genuinely independent rows on one
surface (§6) are the right one.

What the classification says for irrationality proofs. The score log(1/|λ2 |) − k is not a property
of the sequence. By Theorem 7.2 it splits as log |t2 | − 2 − log λ, and neither factor depends on the
accessory parameter or on the period: |t2 | is fixed by the surface together with the choice of which
fibre sits at ∞, and λ by the 2-adic structure of the weight-two form. The score is therefore a motivic
invariant — of the geometry plus the placement — and the classification computes it once and for
all. Combined with Corollary 6.4 (a positive score forces irrational characteristic roots, hence a real
quadratic field, hence no second rational placement), Theorem 7.3 (only finitely many host geometries,
all enumerated) and Corollary 5.4 and Theorem 7.5 (over every box, exactly two rows clear zero, and
both were known), the honest conclusion is that the second-order world is closed. Its two positive-score
rows are not improvable by rearrangement inside the list: the cusp move produces no new rational
data (Theorem 6.3), merging two placements gives µ ≤ 1 + mini σi /δi , i.e. the better of the two with
no convex-combination gain, and the conjugate placements approximate different numbers. A new
irrationality theorem of this shape must come from outside the second-order box — from a higher-rank
host, or from a bound of Bost–Charles/Calegari–Dimitrov–Tang type applied to a whole function
module rather than to a single linear form.
