# The inventory bound: lcm-free functions in a holonomy count are polynomials

*Fable, 2026-09-02. Closes the "richer pure module" scenarios of `CDT_FINDER.md` §4 B–C and §7 (C1), (C2) and of `CATALAN_MU4.md`: the number $u_1$ of admissible functions carrying no lcm layer is $1$ on every host of interest, by the Pólya–Carlson theorem. Companion to `NUMBER_FIELD_HOLONOMY.md` (which settled the normalisation) — together they locate the remaining freedom of the architecture in the single-layer functions and in the conditional orbit, both of which are analysed in `lattice/inventory/` (agent report).*

## 1. Setting

A holonomy count (CDT Theorem 2.5.1 / 7.0.1, in the form of `CDT_FINDER.md` §1) is run in a coordinate $y$ with $y(0)=0$ on the pure module's curve; the admissible functions are $f_i\in K[[y]]$, $K(y)$-linearly independent, of denominator type $n^{e_i}\prod_j[1..b_{i,j}n]$, with $f_i\circ\varphi$ meromorphic on $\mathbf D$. In CDT's descent architecture (their host and every transported one in the finder) $y=x^2/(x-s)$ is the coordinate on the quotient of $\mathbf P^1\setminus\{0,s,\infty\}$ by the involution $x\mapsto sx/(x-s)$, the orbifold has punctures $y=0,\infty$ and a point $y=4s$ of order two, the map $\varphi=h\circ\psi$ is the orbifold uniformisation composed with a Riemann map $\psi$ avoiding the preimages of the extra points $\Sigma^0$, and $|\varphi'(0)|\le256|s|$. An admissible function is holomorphic on $\varphi(\mathbf D)$ on its principal sheet; its singularities there lie in $\{4s\ (\mathbf Z/2\text{ allowed}),\ \infty\}$ — the points of $\Sigma^0$ must be regular on the principal sheet (they are where the conditional function is regular under the hypothesis and singular on other sheets, which is why the map is restricted to a single preimage of them). Consequently the **principal radius of convergence of every admissible function at $y=0$ is at least $R:=4|s|$**, and $R=4/\lambda_2^{\rm norm}$: $R=4$ for CDT's host and every Apéry-perfect host ($\lambda_2$ a unit), $R=1$ for Catalan's level-8 host ($\lambda_2=4$), and for a host over a real quadratic field the two places have radii $R_v=4|s_v|$ with $\prod_vR_v=16|N(s)|=16$ when $s$ is a unit (the $X_1(5)$ symmetric-square host).

The quantity $\tau^\flat=\sigma_m-m^{-2}\sum_ju_j^2b_j$ is the only place where the inventory's arithmetic enters the bound; $u_j$ is the number of functions free of the $j$-th lcm layer. CDT have $u_1=1$ (the constant) and $u_2=3$; the finder's "best inventory" rows assume $u_1=u_2=m/2$.

## 2. Theorem

**Theorem 2.1 (lcm-free functions).** Let $f=\sum a_ny^n\in\Q[[y]]$ be holonomic (a $G$-function) with $n^ea_n\in\Z$ for all $n$ and some fixed $e\ge0$, and let $R$ be its radius of convergence. (a) If $R>1$ then $f$ is a polynomial. (b) If $R=1$ then $\theta^ef$ is a rational function, $\theta=y\,d/dy$; i.e. $f$ is a polynomial plus a $\Q$-combination of depth-one polylogarithm-type functions $\sum_n c_ny^n/n^e$ with $c_n$ eventually periodic, whose singularities all lie on $|y|=1$ and have unipotent (logarithmic) local monodromy. (c) Over a number field $K$ with $n^ea_n\in\mathcal O_K$: if $\prod_v R_v^{d_v}>1$ over the archimedean places then $f$ is a polynomial.

*Proof.* (a) $g:=\theta^ef=\sum n^ea_ny^n$ has integer coefficients and the same radius $R>1$, so $|n^ea_n|\le R^{-n}(1+\varepsilon)^n\to0$, hence $n^ea_n=0$ for large $n$ and $f$ is a polynomial. (b) $g$ has integer coefficients and radius $1$; by Pólya–Carlson it is rational or has $|y|=1$ as a natural boundary; a holonomic function has finitely many singularities, so $g$ is rational, and $f=\theta^{-e}g$ is as described (the partial fractions of a rational function with integral coefficients and radius $1$ have poles at roots of unity, and $\theta^{-e}$ of $y^k/(1-\zeta y)$ is a depth-one polylogarithm). (c) $N_{K/\Q}(n^ea_n)$ is a rational integer of absolute value $\le\prod_v(R_v^{-n}(1+\varepsilon)^n)^{d_v}\to0$, so it vanishes for large $n$. $\square$

**Corollary 2.2 ($u_1=1$).** In the descent architecture on any host with $R=4|s|\ge1$ — CDT's own, every Apéry-perfect host, Catalan's level-$8$ host — every admissible function without an lcm layer is, modulo $K(y)$-linear dependence, the constant. On Catalan's host ($R=1$) the only other candidates are depth-one polylogarithms with a logarithmic singularity at $y=4s=1$, which is not admissible at an orbifold point of order two (only $\mathbf Z/2$ monodromy is allowed there), and rational functions, which are $K(y)$-dependent on $1$. Hence $u_1=1$; the scenarios $u_1=m/2$ of `CDT_FINDER.md` §4 B–C, (C1) and (C2), and the "$>55.4\%$ lcm-free" requirement of `CATALAN_MU4.md` §5, are impossible.

**Corollary 2.3 (rate lower bound).** Every non-polynomial admissible function has denominator rate $\sigma_i\ge\log R$; on an Apéry-perfect host $\log4=1.386$, i.e. at least one full layer in the doubled coordinate ($b_j=2$ means a layer of rate $2$). So the layers are quantised: $u_1=1$, and all other functions carry $\sigma_i\ge2$.

## 3. What remains free: the single-layer functions and the orbit

With $u_1=1$ forced, $\tau^\flat=\sigma_m-m^{-2}(2+2u_2^2)$ for two layers of $b=2$, so the only inventory lever left is $u_2$, the number of functions with exactly one layer. Such functions exist in unbounded number: on the orbifold cover the arcsine module $F_e:=\sum_{n\ge1}y^n/(n^e\binom{2n}n)$ ($e\ge0$; $\binom{2n}n\mid[1..2n]$, so one layer and $n^e$), with $\theta F_e=F_{e-1}$, $F_2=2\arcsin^2(\sqrt y/2)$, all holomorphic on the cover with the allowed $\mathbf Z/2$ at $y=4$ — but each integration raises $e$, and $\tau^\sharp(\mathbf e)=\frac2{m^2}\min_\xi\{\xi\sum e_i+(\max e_i)I^m_\xi(\xi)\}$ grows like $\log(m/4)$ when the $e_i$ run up to $m/2$. Whether some mixture passes entry at the ceiling is a finite optimisation over $(u_2,\mathbf e,m)$ on each host; it is carried out in `lattice/inventory/` for CDT's host (control), Catalan's level-$8$ host and the $X_1(5)$ symmetric square, together with the conditional side (the size of the $\theta$-orbit of the conditional generator and the unconditional "doubly small" functions of `CATALAN_THREE_PERIOD.md`). The expected outcome is that the maximal gain is a few tenths of a nat, since single-layer functions beyond the first two or three cost more in $\tau^\sharp$ than they save in $\tau^\flat$.
