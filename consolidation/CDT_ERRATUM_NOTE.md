# Note on the denominator type of $H_C$ in arXiv:2408.15403 (Lemma 2.11.13 / Theorem 2.11.17)

*Prepared 2026-09-02 for River, to forward to Calegari–Dimitrov–Tang if desired. All numbering refers to arXiv v2 (16 Sep 2024).*

**The claim.** Lemma 2.11.13 ("around $1/4$") defines
$$H_A=\frac1{\sqrt{1-4x}},\quad H_B=\frac1{\sqrt{1-4x}}\int_0^x\frac{dt}{(1-t)\sqrt{1-4t}},\quad H_C=\frac1{\sqrt{1-4x}}\int_0^x\frac{\log(1-t)\,dt}{t\sqrt{1-4t}},\quad H_D=\frac1{\sqrt{1-4x}}\int_0^x\frac{\log(1-t)\,dt}{(1-t)\sqrt{1-4t}}$$
and states that they have denominator types $1$, $[1..n]$, $[1..n][1..n/2]$, $[1..n][1..n/2]$ respectively. Theorem 2.11.17 then deduces from Theorem 2.11.11 (the $[1..n][1..n/2]$ structure theorem with a fourth puncture $\delta$) that $1$, $\pi/\sqrt3$, $\pi^2$, $3L(2,\chi_{-3})-\frac\pi{\sqrt3}\log3$ are $\mathbf Q$-linearly independent.

**The issue.** $H_C$ does not have type $[1..n][1..n/2]$:
$$H_C=-x-\tfrac{13}{4}x^2-\tfrac{197}{18}x^3-\tfrac{5525}{144}x^4-\tfrac{27633}{200}x^5-\cdots$$
Already at $n=2$ the denominator $4$ does not divide $[1,2][1,1]=2$. Exact computation to $n=400$ shows that the excess of $\mathrm{den}(a_n)$ over $[1..n][1..n/2]$ is unbounded — it contains the product of the primes in $(2n/3,n]$ and grows like $e^{n/3}$ — so no constant factor repairs it; the exact type of $H_C$ is $[1..n]^2$. The types claimed for $H_A$, $H_B$, $H_D$ are correct (exact, $n\le400$), and the three monodromy coefficients $L(1,\chi_{-3})$, $-\pi^2/18$, $L(1,\chi_{-3})\log3-L(2,\chi_{-3})$ are correct.

**Why.** Writing $\int_0^xh(t)k(t)\,dt$ with $h\in\mathbf Z[[t]]$ and $k=\sum k_jt^j$: the coefficient of $x^n$ is $\frac1n\sum_{j<n}h_{n-1-j}k_j$. If $[1..j]k_j\in\mathbf Z$ (as for $k=\log(1-t)/(1-t)=-\sum\mathcal H_jt^j$), an elementary $p$-adic count gives type $[1..n][1..n/2]$: the $\frac1n$ and the worst $k_j$ never both attain a full power of $p$ simultaneously. For $k=\log(1-t)/t=-\sum t^j/(j+1)$ the coefficient $k_{p-1}=-1/p$ meets the outer $\frac1p$ at $n=p$, giving $p^2$, and the type is $[1..n]^2$.

**Consequence.** The argument of Theorem 2.11.17 applies to combinations $aH_A+bH_B+dH_D$ (no $H_C$), and proves the three-element statement: $1$, $\pi/\sqrt3$, $3L(2,\chi_{-3})-\frac\pi{\sqrt3}\log3$ are $\mathbf Q$-linearly independent. The clause involving $\pi^2$ is not covered. No function of type $[1..n][1..n/2]$ in the module generated by $H_A$ and kernels $r(t)\log(1-t)$, $r\in\mathbf Q[t,\frac1{1-t}]$, has fold period $\pi^2$: the fold periods of that whole layer span $\mathbf Q+\mathbf Q\pi/\sqrt3+\mathbf Q\bigl(3L(2,\chi_{-3})-\frac\pi{\sqrt3}\log3\bigr)$ (PSLQ, 150 digits, and a structural computation). A positive answer to Question 2.8.3 (the $[1..n]^2$ version with $\delta\in[-\frac12,\frac12]$) would restore the $\pi^2$ clause, since $H_C$ has type $[1..n]^2$ and $\delta=\frac14$ is in range. The Mahler-measure corollary $m\bigl((1+x+y)^4/3\bigr)\notin\mathbf Q$ is unaffected (it uses only $H_A,H_B,H_D$).
