# Cusp periods of modular Apéry systems: one recurrence, many periods

*Fable, 2026-09-02. Theory here; census, monodromy checks and scripts in `lattice/cusp_periods/REPORT.md` (18 scripts, 60–135 digits). Version 1: the draft's Abel-type outer formula and its "mean-zero" polar condition were wrong and are replaced by the unconditional forms of §3; a third regularity condition (§3.3) was found by the census. Motivation: Track D found that Zagier's row **D** has period $\zeta(2)/5$ at its near cusp and $-\tfrac{11}5\zeta(2)$ at its far cusp, while the $\mathbf Q(\sqrt5)$-direction on $\Gamma_1(5)$ realises $\xi$ at the near cusp and $\sigma(\xi)$ at the far cusp of the conjugate source.*

## 1. Setting

A modular Apéry system on a genus-zero group $\Gamma\supseteq\Gamma_1(N)$: a coordinate $x=q+O(q^2)$, a weight-$k$ form $F=\sum A_nx^n$, and a holomorphic source $\Phi=c_0+\sum_{m\ge1}c(m)q^m\in M_{r+1}(\Gamma)$, $r\ge2$. Eichler integral and companion:
$$\Theta:=D^{-r}\Phi=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{c(m)}{m^r}q^m,\qquad B:=F\,\Theta=\sum B_nx^n,\qquad B_n=\sum_{m\le n}\frac{c(m)}{m^r}e_{n,m},\quad e_{n,m}=[x^n](Fq^m),$$
so $d_n^{\,r}B_n\in\mathbf Z$ whenever $c(m)\in\mathbf Z$ (`COMPANION_ARITHMETIC.md` §1). $A$ and $B$ satisfy $\mathcal LA=0$ and $\mathcal LB=R$ with $\mathcal L$ the host's Picard–Fuchs operator and, when $c_0=0$, $R=x\,\Phi/\Phi_{\rm can}$ a rational function ($\Phi_{\rm can}=F\cdot Dx$). The finite singular points of $\mathcal L$ are cusp images $x(\mathfrak c)$ (weight-one hosts: all of them; Fricke hosts: the near one is the fold $x(i/\sqrt N)$, the far one a cusp).

**Definition.** $\Phi$ has a *period at the singular point $x_{\mathfrak c}$* if there is $\Pi\in\mathbf C$ with $B-\Pi F$ analytic there; then $\Pi$ is unique and is called $\Pi_{\mathfrak c}(\Phi)$. At the singularity nearest $0$ it is the Apéry limit $\lim B_n/A_n$, and $A_n\Pi-B_n\to0$ geometrically. If $\mathcal L$ has exponents $0,0$ at $x_{\mathfrak c}$ (a cusp) and $R$ is regular there, a period exists: $B=y_{\rm part}+\alpha u+\beta(u\log+v)$ with $y_{\rm part}$ analytic, and $\Pi=\beta/\beta_F$. If $R$ has a pole at $x_{\mathfrak c}$, $B$ acquires a $\log^2$ and no period exists. Track D computed $\Pi$ at far cusps by exactly this monodromy recipe.

## 2. Orientation decomposition

For $E^{\psi,\varphi}_{r+1}$ with $c(m)=\sum_{d\mid m}\psi(m/d)\varphi(d)d^r$, writing $m=fe$,
$$\Theta=\sum_{f\ge1}\frac{\psi(f)}{f^r}\Lambda_\varphi(q^f),\qquad \Lambda_\varphi(z)=\sum_{e\ge1}\varphi(e)z^e=\frac{N_\varphi(z)}{1-z^{Q}},\quad N_\varphi(z)=\sum_{a=1}^{Q}\varphi(a)z^a,$$
$Q$ the period of $\varphi$. For $\varphi=\mathbf 1$: $\Lambda(z)=z/(1-z)$. Poles of $\Lambda_\varphi$: the $Q$-th roots of unity $z_0$ with $N_\varphi(z_0)\ne0$; for $\varphi\ne\mathbf 1$ the point $z=1$ is removable with $\Lambda_\varphi(1)=-\tfrac1Q\sum a\varphi(a)=-B_{1,\varphi}$. *Inner* orientation $(\psi,\mathbf 1)$: Lambert series $\sum\psi(f)f^{-r}q^f/(1-q^f)$. *Outer* $(\mathbf 1,\varphi)$: $\sum_e\varphi(e)\mathrm{Li}_r(q^e)$. Sources are finite combinations $\Phi=\sum_d c_d\,E^{\psi,\varphi}_{r+1}(d\tau)$.

## 3. Behaviour at a cusp

Cusp $\mathfrak c=a/c$, $\zeta=e(a/c)$, radial approach $q=\zeta e^{-t}$. Near a pole $z_0$ of $\Lambda_\varphi$, $\Lambda_\varphi(z_0e^{-u})=\frac{N_\varphi(z_0)}{Qu}+\bigl[\tfrac12N_\varphi(z_0)-\tfrac1Qz_0N'_\varphi(z_0)\bigr]+O(u)$. Put $w_\varphi(z)=\Lambda_\varphi(z)$ off the poles and $w_\varphi(z_0)=\tfrac12N_\varphi(z_0)-\tfrac1Qz_0N'_\varphi(z_0)$ at a pole; $n_\varphi(z_0)=N_\varphi(z_0)/Q$ at a pole and $0$ elsewhere. For $\varphi=\mathbf 1$: $w(1)=-\tfrac12=\zeta(0)$.

**Theorem 3.1 (expansion at a cusp) [exact; validated 60 digits, Abel cross-check 32 digits].** For $r\ge2$, as $t\to0^+$,
$$\Theta(\zeta e^{-t})=\frac{\rho_{\mathfrak c}}{t}+\Pi^{q}_{\mathfrak c}+O(t\log\tfrac1t),\qquad
\rho_{\mathfrak c}=\sum_dc_dd^{-r-1}\sum_{f\ge1}\frac{\psi(f)}{f^{r+1}}n_\varphi(\zeta^{df}),\qquad
\Pi^{q}_{\mathfrak c}=\sum_dc_dd^{-r}\sum_{f\ge1}\frac{\psi(f)}{f^{r}}w_\varphi(\zeta^{df}).$$
Both sums converge absolutely; no regularisation is needed. Since $w_\varphi(\zeta^{df})$ depends on $f$ modulo $c/\gcd(c,d)$ and $\psi(f)$ modulo $Q_\psi$, with $M=\mathrm{lcm}$ of these,
$$\sum_f\frac{\psi(f)w_\varphi(\zeta^{df})}{f^s}=M^{-s}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\psi(j)\,w_\varphi\bigl(e(adj/c)\bigr)\,\zeta_H(s,j/M),$$
so **every cusp period is a $\mathbf Q(\zeta_M)$-linear combination of $L(r,\chi)$, $\chi$ mod $M=\mathrm{lcm}(c,\mathrm{cond}\,\varphi,\mathrm{cond}\,\psi)$**. Equivalently, by Mellin inversion, $\Pi^q_{\mathfrak c}=A(r)$ and $\rho_{\mathfrak c}=\mathrm{Res}_{s=r+1}A(s)$ for the twisted Dirichlet series $A(s)=\sum c(m)e(am/c)m^{-s}$. In the outer orientation this gives the unconditional form $\Pi^q=\sum_{j=1}^{P}\varphi(j)\mathrm{Li}_r(e(aj/c))(\tfrac12-\tfrac jP)$ and the polar condition $\sum_j\varphi(j)\mathrm{Li}_{r+1}(e(aj/c))=0$ (with $\mathrm{Li}_{r+1}$, not $\mathrm{Li}_r$: the draft's "mean-zero" condition was wrong, and its Abel formula is correct only when that mean happens to vanish, as it does for Zagier D at the cusps $0$ and $1/2$).

**Theorem 3.2 (the polar part is the constant term) [verified 77 digits at every cusp of rows A, C, E, γ, ζ against `mfslashexpansion`].** With $\gamma(\infty)=a/c$,
$$\rho_{\mathfrak c}=\frac{(2\pi i)^{r+1}}{r!\,c^{r+1}}\,a_0\bigl(\Phi|_{r+1}\gamma\bigr).$$
*Proof.* Bol: $\Theta(\gamma\tau')=(c\tau'+d)^{1-r}[D^{-r}(\Phi|\gamma)(\tau')+P_\gamma(\tau')]$, $\deg P_\gamma\le r$ with top coefficient $(2\pi i)^ra_0(\Phi|\gamma)/r!$; along $\tau=a/c+iy$ one has $c\tau'+d=i/(cy)$ and $t=2\pi y$, so the top term contributes $a_0(\Phi|\gamma)(2\pi i)^r\tau'^{\,r}(c\tau')^{1-r}/r!\propto a_0/t$ with the stated constant. $\square$

Consequences. (i) In the inner orientation $\rho_{\mathfrak c}=\sum_dc_dd^{-r-1}\psi(c_d)c_d^{-r-1}L(r+1,\psi)$, $c_d=c/\gcd(c,d)$: automatically zero when $\gcd(c,\mathrm{cond}\,\psi)>1$, otherwise a **critical $L(r+1)$-value condition**. On $\Gamma_1(5)$ at the cusp $0$, for $\alpha_{\psi_4}=1+i\varphi^5$, $\alpha_{\bar\psi_4}=1-i\varphi^5$, it reads $\mathrm{Re}\,L(3,\psi_4)=\varphi^5\,\mathrm{Im}\,L(3,\psi_4)$: Track D's identity is the fold-regularity condition itself. It is proved exactly from $L(3,\chi)=-i(2\pi)^3\tau(\chi)B_{3,\bar\chi}/(12\cdot5^3)$: the ratio is $(2s_1+s_2)/(2s_2-s_1)$ with $s_1/s_2=2\cos(\pi/5)=\varphi$, hence $\varphi^3\varphi^2=\varphi^5$ (`03_L3exact.gp`; note the naive $B_{3,\mathrm{Re}}/B_{3,\mathrm{Im}}=2$ is not the $L$-ratio). (ii) In the outer orientation $\rho_{\mathfrak c}\ne0$ only at cusps where $\zeta^{df}$ reaches a pole of $\Lambda_\varphi$, i.e. where $\mathrm{cond}\,\varphi$ divides $c$ up to $\gcd$'s; on $\Gamma_1(5)$ only at $2/5$.

**Proposition 3.3 (third regularity condition) [verified, exact rationals to $n=300$].** The companion $B=F\cdot D^{-r}\Phi_{\ge1}$ has a period at the near cusp only if, in addition to $\rho_{\rm near}=0$, **$a_0(\Phi,\infty)=0$**. *Reason.* $\mathcal LB=x(\Phi-a_0)/\Phi_{\rm can}$; the piece $x\Phi/\Phi_{\rm can}$ is a modular function, hence rational in $x$, but $x\,a_0/\Phi_{\rm can}$ has weight $-(r+1)$ and is not; it is singular where $\Phi_{\rm can}$ vanishes, i.e. at the cusp over the near singularity. Equivalently, the genuine Eichler integral is $\Theta+a_0(2\pi i\tau)^r/r!$, and $F\cdot\tau^r$ is not analytic in $x$ at any cusp image other than $x=0$. On $\Gamma_1(5)$ the two outer directions $R_1=2E_3^{\mathbf 1,\mathrm{re}\psi_4}$, $R_2=-2E_3^{\mathbf 1,\mathrm{im}\psi_4}$ have $\rho=0$ at both cusps $0$ and $1/2$ and $q$-side periods $\tfrac65\zeta(2)$, $-\tfrac25\zeta(2)$, yet $B_n/A_n$ misses them by defects in the exact ratio $-2=c_0(R_1)/c_0(R_2)$, and the inhomogeneity $R_n$ grows like $\varphi^{5n}$; only $\tfrac12R_1+R_2=\Phi_D$, with $c_0=0$, has a period. This is `hostscan` §10.3's observation and the `eis` census's "constant term zero at both $0$ and $\infty$", derived from the other side. So: **fold-regular subspace $=\{a_0(\infty)=0\}\cap\{a_0(\text{near cusp})=0\}$**, of dimension $4-2=2$ on $\Gamma_1(5)$ and $\Gamma_0(6)$.

## 4. The period formula and the orientation dichotomy

**Theorem 4.1 (cusp periods).** If $a_0(\Phi,\infty)=0$ and $\rho_{\mathfrak c}=0$, then $\Pi_{\mathfrak c}(\Phi)=\Pi^q_{\mathfrak c}(\Phi)$ of Theorem 3.1, i.e. the monodromy period equals the twisted $L$-value $A(r)$. *[verified 100–135 digits by analytic continuation of the exact companion series on rows A, C, E, F, D, α, with the dictionary $x(\mathfrak c)$: A $0\mapsto\tfrac18$, $\tfrac12\mapsto-1$; C $0\mapsto\tfrac19$, $\tfrac13\mapsto1$; E $0\mapsto\tfrac18$, $\tfrac14\mapsto\tfrac14$; F $0\mapsto\tfrac19$, $\tfrac16\mapsto\tfrac18$; D $0\mapsto\varphi^{-5}$, $\tfrac12\mapsto-\varphi^5$; α $0\mapsto\tfrac1{16}$, $\tfrac14\mapsto\tfrac14$; imaginary parts included.]* At cusps where $\rho\ne0$ the number $A(r)$ still exists but is not a period.

*Caveat (representatives).* $\Pi^q$ is a function of the rational $a/c$, not of its $\Gamma$-class: $\Pi^q(1/N)=L(\Phi,r)\ne0=\Pi^q(\infty)$ although $1/N\sim\infty$, because $\Theta$ is not modular and shifts by period polynomials. The monodromy period selects the representative that the radial path in the $x$-disc actually reaches; the natural conjecture $\Pi^q(\gamma\mathfrak c)-\Pi^q(\mathfrak c)=P_\gamma$ evaluated at the cusp is untested.

**Theorem 4.2 (orientation dichotomy) [exact].** (i) *Inner*: $w(z)=z/(1-z)$ has $\mathrm{Re}\,w(e^{i\theta})=-\tfrac12$ for every $\theta\ne0$ and $\mathrm{Im}\,w=\tfrac12\cot(\theta/2)$, and $w(1)=\zeta(0)=-\tfrac12$. Hence at **every** cusp
$$\mathrm{Re}\,\Pi_{\mathfrak c}=-\tfrac12\sum_dc_dd^{-r}L(r,\psi)=L(\Phi,r),\qquad \mathrm{Im}\,\Pi_{\mathfrak c}=\tfrac12\sum_dc_dd^{-r}\sum_f\frac{\psi(f)}{f^r}\cot\frac{\pi adf}{c}.$$
The orientation factor $\mathrm{Re}\,\Pi_{\mathfrak c}/L(\Phi,r)$ is $+1$ at every cusp, the formula is $K$-linear in the coefficients, and therefore **for a $K$-rational inner source and $\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}$, $\Pi_{\mathfrak c}(\sigma\Phi)=\sigma\bigl(\Pi_{\mathfrak c'}(\Phi)\bigr)$ formally at any two cusps where both are fold-regular.** (ii) *Outer*: $\mathrm{Re}\,\Lambda_\varphi$ is not constant on the circle; at $c=2$, $\Pi_{1/2}/\Pi_0=[2^{-r}\Lambda_\varphi(1)+(1-2^{-r})\Lambda_\varphi(-1)]/\Lambda_\varphi(1)$. Zagier D ($\varphi=(1,-2,2,-1,0)$, $r=2$): $\Lambda(1)=\tfrac15$, $\Lambda(-1)=-3$, factor $(\tfrac1{20}-\tfrac94)/\tfrac15=-11$. Zagier A ($\chi_{-3}$, oldform $1-V_2$): factor $-3$. In polylog language, $\mathrm{Li}_r(-1)=-(1-2^{1-r})\mathrm{Li}_r(1)$.

## 5. The census (`lattice/cusp_periods/REPORT.md` §§5–9)

* **Near-fold periods re-derived from the cusp formula alone**, all twelve ledger rows: $\zeta(2)/4$ (A), $L(2,\chi_{-3})/2$ (B, C), $\zeta(2)/5$ (D), $G/2$ (E), $5L(2,\chi_{-3})/8$ (F), $7\zeta(3)/24$ (α), $\zeta(3)/6$ (γ), $13\zeta(3)/54$ (δ), $7\zeta(3)/32$ (ε), $L(3,\chi_{-3})/3$ (ζ), $L(3,\chi_5)/2$ (η) — an independent re-derivation of Theorem B's value column.
* **All cusps.** Orientation factor $\ne1$ occurs only on A ($-3$ at $1/2$), D ($-11$ at $1/2$) and ζ ($-\tfrac12$ at $1/3,2/3$, non-regular), exactly the sources with $\varphi\ne\mathbf 1$; every inner row (B, C, E, F, η) and every trivial/trivial row has factor $1$ everywhere, with imaginary parts that are rational multiples of $\pi^r$, $\pi^r/\sqrt3$, $\pi^r/\sqrt5$ (tables in the report).
* **Folds are not cusps.** For γ, ε, ζ the far singularity is a fold; its "period" is complex — $\zeta(3)/6+2\pi^3i/15$, $7\zeta(3)/32+\pi^3i/24$, and $-\tfrac72\cdot L(3,\chi_{-3})/3$ — with no $q$-side derivation here (the fold analogue of §3 is a $W_N$-twisted Mellin split, not done).
* **Galois.** $\Phi_{\rm new}$ realises $\xi$, and $\sigma\Phi_{\rm new}$ realises $\sigma(\xi)$, at every cusp of $\Gamma_1(5)$ to $10^{-77}$ (factor $+1$), against $-11$ on the $\zeta(2)$-line of the same host. At weight four on $\Gamma_1(5)$ all four Eisenstein directions, all polar coefficients and the fold-regular subspace are $\mathbf Q$-rational: Beukers' $\sqrt5$ is geometry, not Eisenstein bookkeeping, and weight three is the only place where $\varphi^5$ is forced.
* **Cooper's meromorphic rows** (continuation only): far-fold factors $-5$ ($s_7$), $-2$ ($s_{10}$), $+1$ ($s_{18}$), matching $\psi=\mathbf 1,\mathbf 1,\chi_{-3}$ — the dichotomy survives meromorphy; $s_{18}$'s far imaginary part $\pi^2/(18\sqrt3)$ equals Zagier F's to 100 digits.

## 6. Consequences

* *Track D's hypothesis tax is an orientation theorem.* A two-place construction over a real quadratic field has a Galois-equivariant hypothesis iff the source is inner at the two cusps involved. The $\zeta(2)$-line on $\Gamma_1(5)$ is outer (factor $-11$); the $\xi$-line is inner (factor $1$). The search criterion of `GAMMA15_CLOSURE.md` §5 is therefore: an Apéry-perfect host whose fold-regular space is spanned by inner directions at both the near and the far cusp.
* *Fold-regularity is computable exactly:* it is the joint kernel of the constant-term functionals at $\infty$ and at the near cusp, all of which are algebraic multiples of $\pi^{r+1}$ (critical values). The far-cusp period of a fold-regular source is a twisted $L(r)$-value with an explicit root-of-unity weight, which is all the Diophantine information the far cusp carries.
* *One recurrence, many periods:* each fold-regular cusp gives an exact linear form $A_n\Pi_{\mathfrak c}-B^{(\mathfrak c)}_n$ with the same lcm denominators; only the near one converges.

## 7. Cusp-form sources (`lattice/cuspform_sources/REPORT.md`; note `CUSPFORM_SOURCES.md`)

Fricke hosts, $r=3$: a source $\Phi\in M_4(\Gamma_0(N))$ with $a_0=0$ is fold-regular at the near fold **iff $\Phi|_4W_N=-\Phi$**, and then $\xi=L(\Phi,3)$; the period-polynomial conditions are automatic from the functional equation. No newform of level $5$–$9$ has Fricke sign $-1$ at weight four, so no cusp form works on either Apéry-perfect host; five oldform combinations $f-d^2f(d\tau)$ work on non-perfect hosts (Domb, $s_{10}$, $s_{18}$, level-12), giving Apéry-like forms in $1$ and $L(f,3)$ for $f=5.4.a.a,\,6.4.a.a,\,9.4.a.a$ with $k=3$, geometric rate, and $K_-=L(f,2)/\sqrt\pi$ (the central value). The Apéry limit does not see the host: $\xi=\Theta(q_c)+(2\pi/\sqrt N)D\Theta(q_c)$, $q_c=e^{-2\pi/\sqrt N}$. No irrationality (deficit $4.4$ nats).
