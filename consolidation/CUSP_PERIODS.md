# Cusp periods of modular Apéry systems: one recurrence, many periods

*Fable, 2026-09-02. DRAFT: the theory; the census (`lattice/cusp_periods/REPORT.md`, in progress) will confirm or correct it. Motivation: Track D found that Zagier's row **D** has period $\zeta(2)/5$ at its near cusp and $-\tfrac{11}{5}\zeta(2)$ at its far cusp, while the new $\mathbf Q(\sqrt5)$-direction on $\Gamma_1(5)$ has $\xi$ at the near cusp and the Galois conjugate $\sigma(\xi)$ at the far cusp of the conjugate source. This note explains both and gives the general formula.*

## 1. Setting

A modular Apéry system on a genus-zero group $\Gamma\supseteq\Gamma_1(N)$: a Hauptmodul-type coordinate $x=q+O(q^2)$, a weight-$k$ form $F=\sum A_nx^n$, and an Eisenstein source $\Phi\in M_{r+1}(\Gamma)$, $\Phi=c_0+\sum_{m\ge1}c(m)q^m$, $r\ge2$. The Eichler integral and the companion are
$$D^{-r}\Phi:=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{c(m)}{m^r}q^m,\qquad B:=F\cdot D^{-r}\Phi=\sum B_nx^n,\qquad B_n=\sum_{m\le n}\frac{c(m)}{m^r}e_{n,m},\ e_{n,m}=[x^n](Fq^m)$$
(`COMPANION_ARITHMETIC.md` §1). $A$ and $B$ satisfy $\mathcal L A=0$, $\mathcal L B=R(x)$ with $\mathcal L$ the Picard–Fuchs operator of the host and $R$ rational. The singular points of $\mathcal L$ are the images $x(\mathfrak c)$ of the cusps (and, in the Fricke family, of the Fricke fixed point); at a cusp image $x_{\mathfrak c}$ the local exponents are $0,0$ for $k=1$ and the local solutions are $u_{\mathfrak c}$ (analytic) and $u_{\mathfrak c}\log(x-x_{\mathfrak c})+v_{\mathfrak c}$.

**Definition.** $\Phi$ is *fold-regular at the cusp $\mathfrak c$* if $R$ is regular at $x_{\mathfrak c}$. Then there is a unique $\Pi_{\mathfrak c}(\Phi)\in\mathbf C$ with $B-\Pi_{\mathfrak c}(\Phi)\,F$ analytic at $x_{\mathfrak c}$ (the log-coefficient ratio), and we call it the *period of $\Phi$ at $\mathfrak c$*. When $\mathfrak c$ is the singularity nearest $0$, $\Pi_{\mathfrak c}$ is the Apéry limit $\lim B_n/A_n$. If $R$ has a pole at $x_{\mathfrak c}$, $B$ acquires a $\log^2$ there and no such number exists.

*Why a unique $\Pi$ exists (sketch).* $R$ regular at $x_{\mathfrak c}$ and exponents $0,0$ give an analytic particular solution of $\mathcal Ly=R$ near $x_{\mathfrak c}$ (the indicial factor $j^2$ never vanishes for $j\ge1$), so $B=y_{\rm part}+\alpha u_{\mathfrak c}+\beta(u_{\mathfrak c}\log+v_{\mathfrak c})$, and $B-\Pi F$ is analytic iff $\beta=\Pi\beta_F$. This is the "homogeneous-solution obstruction" that Track D computed by monodromy; it is a single scalar condition, always solvable when there is no $\log^2$.

## 2. Orientation decomposition of the Eichler integral

For $E_{r+1}^{\psi,\varphi}$ with $c(m)=\sum_{d\mid m}\psi(m/d)\varphi(d)d^{r}$ (conductors $f_\psi,f_\varphi$), writing $m=ed$,
$$D^{-r}E^{\psi,\varphi}_{r+1}=\sum_{e,d\ge1}\psi(e)e^{-r}\varphi(d)q^{ed}=\sum_{e\ge1}\psi(e)e^{-r}\,\Lambda_\varphi(q^e),\qquad \Lambda_\varphi(z):=\sum_{d\ge1}\varphi(d)z^d=\frac{N_\varphi(z)}{1-z^{f}},\ N_\varphi(z)=\sum_{a=1}^{f}\varphi(a)z^a,\ f=f_\varphi.$$
Equivalently $=\sum_d\varphi(d)\,\mathrm{Li}_r^{\psi}(q^d)$ with $\mathrm{Li}^\psi_r(z)=\sum_e\psi(e)z^e/e^r$. The two extreme orientations are
$$\text{outer }(\mathbf 1,\varphi):\ \sum_d\varphi(d)\mathrm{Li}_r(q^d)\ (\text{polylogarithms}),\qquad \text{inner }(\psi,\mathbf 1):\ \sum_e\psi(e)e^{-r}\frac{q^e}{1-q^e}\ (\text{Lambert series}).$$
$\Lambda_\varphi$ is a rational function whose poles are the $f$-th roots of unity $z_0$ with $N_\varphi(z_0)\ne0$ (for primitive $\varphi$: exactly the primitive $f$-th roots, $N_\varphi(\zeta_f^b)=\bar\varphi(b)\tau(\varphi)$); $z=1$ is a removable singularity when $\varphi\ne\mathbf 1$, with $\Lambda_\varphi(1)=-\tfrac1f\sum_{a=1}^f a\varphi(a)=-B_{1,\varphi}$. For $\varphi=\mathbf 1$: $\Lambda_{\mathbf 1}(z)=z/(1-z)$, one pole at $z=1$.

## 3. Behaviour at a cusp

Let $\mathfrak c=a/c$ (lowest terms), $\zeta=e^{2\pi ia/c}$, and approach the cusp radially: $q=\zeta e^{-t}$, $t\to0^+$. Near a pole $z_0$ of $\Lambda_\varphi$, with $z=z_0e^{-s}$,
$$\Lambda_\varphi(z_0e^{-s})=\frac{N_\varphi(z_0e^{-s})}{1-e^{-fs}}=\frac{N_\varphi(z_0)}{fs}+\Bigl[\frac{N_\varphi(z_0)}2-\frac{z_0N_\varphi'(z_0)}{f}\Bigr]+O(s).$$
Put $w_\varphi(z)=\Lambda_\varphi(z)$ off the poles and $w_\varphi(z_0)=\tfrac12N_\varphi(z_0)-\tfrac1fz_0N'_\varphi(z_0)$ at a pole (for $\varphi=\mathbf 1$: $w_{\mathbf 1}(1)=-\tfrac12$).

**Proposition 3.1 (polar part and period).** As $t\to0^+$, for $r\ge2$,
$$D^{-r}E^{\psi,\varphi}_{r+1}(\zeta e^{-t})=\frac{p_{\mathfrak c}}{t}+\Pi^{q}_{\mathfrak c}+o(1),\qquad p_{\mathfrak c}=\frac1f\sum_{e:\ \zeta^e\in\mathrm{Poles}(\Lambda_\varphi)}\psi(e)\,e^{-r-1}N_\varphi(\zeta^e),\qquad \Pi^{q}_{\mathfrak c}=\sum_{e\ge1}\psi(e)\,e^{-r}\,w_\varphi(\zeta^e).$$
*Proof.* Split the $e$-sum at $e\approx1/t$. For $e>1/t$, $|q^e|\le e^{-1}$ keeps $q^e$ away from the poles, so those terms are $O(\sum_{e>1/t}e^{-r})=O(t^{r-1})$. For $e<1/t$ use the expansion above with $s=et$; the error is $O(t\sum_{e<1/t}e^{1-r})$, which is $O(t)$ for $r\ge3$ and $O(t\log(1/t))$ for $r=2$. The polar and constant terms are the stated sums, which converge absolutely for $r\ge2$. $\square$

**Proposition 3.2 (the polar part is the constant term).** $p_{\mathfrak c}\ne0$ iff $\Phi$ has a nonzero constant term at $\mathfrak c$, iff $R$ has a pole at $x_{\mathfrak c}$, iff $B$ has a $\log^2$ at $x_{\mathfrak c}$ (for $k=1$; $\log^{r}$-type in general, `ASYMPTOTIC_CONSTANTS.md` Thm 5.2). *Sketch.* With $\tau=\gamma\tau'$, $\gamma(\infty)=\mathfrak c$, Bol gives $D^{-r}\Phi(\tau)=(c\tau'+d)^{1-r}\bigl[D^{-r}(\Phi|_{r+1}\gamma)(\tau')+P_\gamma(\tau')\bigr]$ with $P_\gamma$ a polynomial of degree $\le r$ whose degree-$r$ coefficient is a nonzero multiple of the constant term $a_0(\Phi|\gamma)$; since $\tau'\asymp1/t$, the $1/t$ term of the expansion is exactly $a_0(\Phi|\gamma)\tau'^{\,r}(c\tau')^{1-r}\propto a_0/t$. The other equivalences are the cusp-constant theorem. $\square$

**Examples.** *Inner orientation at a cusp $a/c$:* the poles are at $\zeta^e=1$, i.e. $c\mid e$, so $p_{\mathfrak c}=\sum_{c\mid e}\psi(e)e^{-r-1}=\psi(c)\,c^{-r-1}L(r+1,\psi)$, which vanishes automatically when $\gcd(c,f_\psi)>1$ and is a **critical** $L$-value otherwise. Hence an inner direction is never fold-regular on its own at a cusp with $\gcd(c,f_\psi)=1$; a combination $\sum_\psi\alpha_\psi E^{\psi,\mathbf 1}$ is fold-regular there iff $\sum_\psi\alpha_\psi\psi(c)L(r+1,\psi)=0$. On $\Gamma_1(5)$ with $\psi\in\{\psi_4,\bar\psi_4\}$ and $\alpha=(1+i\varphi^5,1-i\varphi^5)$ at $c=1$ this reads $\mathrm{Re}\,L(3,\psi_4)=\varphi^5\mathrm{Im}\,L(3,\psi_4)$ — the identity found in Track D, now seen as the fold-regularity condition itself, and as an exact statement about generalised Bernoulli numbers $B_{3,\psi_4}$ (both sides are $\pi^3$ times algebraic numbers). *Outer orientation:* $p_{\mathfrak c}\ne0$ only at cusps where some $\zeta^e$ is a primitive $f_\varphi$-th root of unity, i.e. $f_\varphi\mid c\cdot(\ldots)$; on $\Gamma_1(5)$ that is the cusp $2/5$ only, and there $p=\tfrac15\sum_{5\nmid e}e^{-r-1}N_\varphi(\zeta^e)$, a Gauss-sum multiple of $L(r+1,\bar\varphi)$.

## 4. The period formula and its consequences

**Theorem 4.1 (cusp periods).** If $\Phi=\sum\alpha_{\psi,\varphi}E^{\psi,\varphi}_{r+1}$ is fold-regular at $\mathfrak c=a/c$, its period there is
$$\Pi_{\mathfrak c}(\Phi)=\sum_{\psi,\varphi}\alpha_{\psi,\varphi}\sum_{e\ge1}\psi(e)\,e^{-r}\,w_\varphi(\zeta^e),\qquad\zeta=e^{2\pi ia/c}.$$
Since $w_\varphi(\zeta^e)$ depends on $e$ only modulo $P=\mathrm{lcm}(c,f_\varphi)$, with $P'=\mathrm{lcm}(P,f_\psi)$,
$$\Pi_{\mathfrak c}(\Phi)=\sum_{b\bmod P'}g(b)\,P'^{-r}\zeta\bigl(r,\tfrac b{P'}\bigr),\qquad g(b)=\sum\alpha_{\psi,\varphi}\psi(b)w_\varphi(\zeta^b),$$
a $\mathbf Q(\zeta_{P'},\alpha)$-linear combination of Hurwitz values, equivalently of $L(r,\chi)$ for Dirichlet characters $\chi$ modulo $P'$.

*Proof.* The $q$-side constant term $\Pi^q_{\mathfrak c}$ of Prop. 3.1 equals the log-coefficient ratio $\Pi_{\mathfrak c}$ when $p_{\mathfrak c}=0$: in the Bol expansion, $D^{-r}\Phi=(c\tau'+d)^{1-r}[P_\gamma(\tau')+O(Q)]$ with $\deg P_\gamma\le r-1$, and the coefficient of $\tau'^{\,0}$ after expanding $(c\tau'+d)^{1-r}$ is the same number that multiplies $\log(x-x_{\mathfrak c})$ in $B$ relative to $F$ (both are the leading coefficient $p_{r-1}/c^{r-1}$ of $P_\gamma$). When $p_{\mathfrak c}\ne0$ the two differ by a multiple of $p_{\mathfrak c}d/c$, and neither is a period. The Hurwitz form is the finite Fourier rearrangement of the $e$-sum. $\square$

**Corollary 4.2 (which values occur).** Every cusp period of a weight-$(r+1)$ Eisenstein system of level $N$ lies in the $\mathbf Q(\zeta_{N})$-span of $\{\zeta(r)\}\cup\{L(r,\chi):\chi\bmod N\}$, and the near-fold Apéry limit is the special case $\mathfrak c=$ the near cusp.

**Theorem 4.3 (orientation dichotomy).**
(i) *Inner directions are cusp-independent and Galois-equivariant.* For $\varphi=\mathbf 1$, $w_{\mathbf 1}(\zeta^e)=\zeta^e/(1-\zeta^e)$ for $\zeta^e\ne1$ and $-\tfrac12$ for $\zeta^e=1$. At any cusp with $c\mid 2$ (i.e. $\zeta=\pm1$) both values are $-\tfrac12$, so $\Pi_{\mathfrak c}(\Phi)=-\tfrac12\sum_\psi\alpha_\psi L(r,\psi)$ is literally the same expression at the cusps $0$ and $1/2$, and it is $K$-linear in the coefficients $\alpha_\psi$: for a source with coefficients in a number field $K$ and $\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}$, $\Pi_{\mathfrak c}(\sigma\Phi)=\sigma\bigl(\Pi_{\mathfrak c'}(\Phi)\bigr)$ formally (apply $\sigma$ to the $\alpha$'s), whenever both sides are fold-regular.
(ii) *Outer directions pick up a root-of-unity weight.* For $\psi=\mathbf 1$, $\Pi_{\mathfrak c}=\sum_e e^{-r}w_\varphi(\zeta^e)=\zeta(r)\sum_{j\bmod c}c^{-r}\,\zeta_H\!\bigl(r,\tfrac jc\bigr)/\zeta(r)\cdot w_\varphi(\zeta^j)$; at $\zeta=-1$ this is $\zeta(r)\bigl[2^{-r}\Lambda_\varphi(1)+(1-2^{-r})\Lambda_\varphi(-1)\bigr]$, which is not the value at $\zeta=1$, $\zeta(r)\Lambda_\varphi(1)$. Equivalently, in the polylog form: $\mathrm{Li}_r(1)=\zeta(r)$ but $\mathrm{Li}_r(-1)=-(1-2^{1-r})\zeta(r)$, so odd and even $d$ are weighted differently.

**Worked example (Zagier D, $r=2$, $\Gamma_1(5)$, cusps $\infty,0,\tfrac12,\tfrac25\mapsto x=0,\varphi^{-5},-\varphi^5,\infty$).** $\Phi_D=(\tfrac12+i)E^{\mathbf 1,\psi_4}_3+(\tfrac12-i)E^{\mathbf 1,\bar\psi_4}_3$, outer with weight $w=\mathrm{re}\,\psi_4-2\,\mathrm{im}\,\psi_4=(1,-2,2,-1,0)$ on $d\bmod5$. $\Lambda_w(1)=-\tfrac15(1-4+6-4)=\tfrac15$, $\Lambda_w(-1)=\tfrac12N_w(-1)=\tfrac12(-1-2-2-1)=-3$. Hence
$$\Pi_0=\zeta(2)\cdot\tfrac15,\qquad \Pi_{1/2}=\zeta(2)\bigl[\tfrac14\cdot\tfrac15+\tfrac34\cdot(-3)\bigr]=-\tfrac{11}5\,\zeta(2),$$
reproducing Track D's monodromy values by hand. For $\Phi_{\rm new}=(1+i\varphi^5)E^{\psi_4,\mathbf 1}+(1-i\varphi^5)E^{\bar\psi_4,\mathbf 1}$ (inner): polar part at cusp $0$ is $\propto(1+i\varphi^5)L(3,\psi_4)+(1-i\varphi^5)L(3,\bar\psi_4)=2(\mathrm{Re}L-\varphi^5\mathrm{Im}L)(3)=0$ (fold-regular), period $\Pi_0=-\tfrac12[(1+i\varphi^5)L(2,\psi_4)+(1-i\varphi^5)L(2,\bar\psi_4)]=\varphi^5\mathrm{Im}L(2,\psi_4)-\mathrm{Re}L(2,\psi_4)=\xi$; at cusp $\tfrac12$ the polar part is $\propto\psi_4(2)(1+i\varphi^5)L(3,\psi_4)+\bar\psi_4(2)(1-i\varphi^5)L(3,\bar\psi_4)=-2(\mathrm{Im}L+\varphi^5\mathrm{Re}L)(3)\ne0$: not fold-regular. The conjugate source $\sigma\Phi_{\rm new}$ (coefficients $1\mp i\varphi^{-5}$) has polar part at $\tfrac12$ equal to $-2\mathrm{Im}L(3)+2\varphi^{-5}\mathrm{Re}L(3)=0$ and period $\Pi_{1/2}(\sigma\Phi_{\rm new})=-\mathrm{Re}L(2,\psi_4)-\varphi^{-5}\mathrm{Im}L(2,\psi_4)=\sigma(\xi)$, exactly as Track D measured. So: on the inner line the far cusp of the conjugate source realises the conjugate period *exactly*; on the outer line the far cusp carries the extra factor $-11$, which is $\tfrac{-11/5}{1/5}$, a pure consequence of $\mathrm{Li}_2(-1)=-\tfrac12\mathrm{Li}_2(1)$ and the character sums $\sum jw(j)=-1$, $\sum j\,w(j)g(j)=22$ over the period $10$.

## 6. Consequences if confirmed

* *One recurrence, many periods.* The single integer sequence $A_n$ of a host carries, for each fold-regular cusp $\mathfrak c$ and each source, a linear form $A_n\Pi_{\mathfrak c}-B^{(\mathfrak c)}_n$ (with $B^{(\mathfrak c)}$ the companion normalised at $\mathfrak c$, i.e. the solution of $\mathcal Ly=R$ regular at the other cusps), with the same lcm denominators. The far-cusp forms converge at the rate $|x_{\mathfrak c}|^{-1}/|x_{\rm near}|^{-1}$ reversed, i.e. they are useless Diophantinely, but they are exact identities between the monodromy of $\mathcal L$ and twisted $L$-values.
* *The hypothesis tax of Track D is now a theorem about orientations:* a two-place construction over a real quadratic field has an equivariant hypothesis iff the source is inner at both cusps involved. The search criterion "a host whose outer direction has uniform cusp weights" means: a host whose fold-regular Eisenstein space is spanned by inner directions at both the near and the far cusp.
* *Fold-regularity is a critical-value condition* (Prop. 3.2 with the inner example): the fold-regular subspace is the kernel of finitely many functionals $\Phi\mapsto\sum\alpha_\psi\psi(c)L(r+1,\psi)$ and $\Phi\mapsto\sum\alpha_\varphi\tau(\varphi)\ldots L(r+1,\bar\varphi)$, all algebraic multiples of $\pi^{r+1}$; so it can be computed exactly, which is how `lattice/hostscan/eis/` found the dimensions $2$ at levels $5$ and $6$.
