## 1. The bound, derived in one page

**Data.** $m$ power series $f_1,\dots,f_m\in\mathbb Q[[x]]$, $\mathbb Q(x)$-linearly independent, with denominator types $[1..b_{i1}n]\cdots[1..b_{ir}n]$ (row sums $\sigma_i$, columns nondecreasing in $i$), and a holomorphic $\varphi:(\mathbb D,0)\to(\mathbb C,0)$ such that every $f_i\circ\varphi$ is meromorphic on $|z|<1$.

**Step 1 (auxiliary function).** Choose polynomials $Q_i\in\mathbb Z[x]$ of degree $<D$ and set $F=\sum Q_if_i$. There are $N=mD$ integer unknowns. Demand $F=\beta x^n+O(x^{n+1})$ with $n=(1-\varepsilon)N$ — that is $n$ homogeneous linear conditions with coefficients of size $e^{O(n)}$ (after clearing the denominators of the $f_i$ up to order $n$). Siegel's lemma gives a nonzero solution with $\log|Q_i|\ll \varepsilon^{-1}\cdot$(size of coefficients)$\,= o(n)$ as $m\to\infty$ at fixed $\varepsilon$ — this is the Thue trick: many more unknowns than equations makes the auxiliary polynomials subexponentially small.

**Step 2 (Liouville).** $\beta\ne0$ is rational; its denominator divides the product of the denominator types at order $n$, so by the prime number theorem $\log|\beta|\ge-\tau n+o(n)$. The crude $\tau$ is $\sigma_m$ (the worst row); the refined $\tau(\mathbf b)=\frac1{m^2}\sum_i(2i-1)\sigma_i$ comes from ordering the functions by denominator quality and noting that the leading monomial of $F$ is, in the high-dimensional version, a *random* monomial whose expected denominator is this weighted average — "denominator rearrangement". Functions with no denominators (pure, algebraic) are free in this average: that is the $\tau^\flat$ effect.

**Step 3 (Cauchy).** $G(z):=z^{-n}F(\varphi(z))$ is holomorphic (after multiplying by a fixed $h$ clearing poles) on the unit disc, with $|G(0)|=|\varphi'(0)|^n|\beta|\ge e^{(\log|\varphi'(0)|-\tau)n}$. On $|z|=1$, $|F\circ\varphi|\le\sum|Q_i(\varphi)|\,|f_i(\varphi)|\le e^{D\max_{\mathbb T}\log^+|\varphi|+o(n)}$. Maximum principle: $(\log|\varphi'(0)|-\tau)\,n\le D\,\max\log^+|\varphi|$, i.e. with $n\approx mD$,
$$m\le\frac{\max_{\mathbb T}\log^+|\varphi|}{\log|\varphi'(0)|-\tau}.$$
CDT's refinement replaces $\max\log^+|\varphi|$ by the Bost–Charles double integral $\iint\log|\varphi(z)-\varphi(w)|$ (or the rearrangement integral $\int_0^12t(\log|\varphi|)^*dt$): the auxiliary polynomial's values on the circle are not all maximal at once; in $d$ auxiliary variables the points equidistribute (concentration of measure) and a Vandermonde factor kills the exceptional set. **That is the whole proof.** Everything else is bookkeeping of which functions are admissible and what $\varphi$ is allowed.

**The two conditions.** *Entry:* $\log|\varphi'(0)|>\tau$ — otherwise $\beta$ is not forced to be large and nothing follows. *Margin:* the exhibited dimension $m$ must exceed the bound. Irrationality proofs exhibit $m$ functions *contingent* on the hypothesis (Apéry limit rational ⟹ the conditional combination $H=bB-aA$ has rational coefficients and is regular at the fold) and contradict the bound.

## 2. Why the fold is the only place the hypothesis enters

Under the hypothesis $\xi=a/b$, the function $H=bB-aA\in\mathbb Q[[x]]$ has denominators of type $[1..n]^{w+1}$ and — this is Beukers' observation — trivial monodromy at the first singularity $t_1$ (the fold), because the period of the extension class at that cusp is exactly $\xi$. So $H$ lives on $\mathbb P^1\setminus(\Sigma\setminus\{t_1\})$ while the pure functions live on $\mathbb P^1\setminus\Sigma$. The map $\varphi$ may therefore be chosen to avoid one point fewer, which is the entire gain. In our language: fold-regularity is the statement $\alpha(\Phi)=0$ on the Eichler period polynomial at the fold cusp (plus, at level 16, the vanishing of the $\log^2$ term), and every such class with nonzero period is a conditional function; every such class with zero period is an *unconditional* "doubly-small" function (`CATALAN_THREE_PERIOD.md`).

## 3. Why CDT's host works: the numerology of $L(2,\chi_{-3})$

Host $X_0(6)$, $x$ with cusps at $0,\tfrac19,1,\infty$. Fold $\tfrac19$ (removed by the hypothesis), outer singularity at $1$. Denominators $[1..n]^2$: $\tau\approx2$. The Kodaira map on $\mathbb P^1\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}$ has $|\varphi'(0)|=16>e^2$: entry passes ($\log16-2=0.77$). The exhibited space: pure polylogarithm-type functions on $\mathbb P^1\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}$ (with better denominators, cheap in $\tau$) plus the $\theta$-orbit of the single conditional generator $\mathcal G=\mathrm{Sym}^+(aH_A+bH_B+cH_C)$, symmetrised under the involution $x\mapsto x/(x-1)$ of $X_0(6)$ to descend to a $y$-line where the geometry is "three points plus one elliptic point", which improves $\varphi$. Total $m=14$ against a bound of $13.99$. The three periods $1,\zeta(2),L$ give a single generator (`CATALAN_THREE_PERIOD.md` Task 2), so the conclusion is linear independence of all three for free. They needed all of: the multivalent map, the symmetrisation, the rearrangement $\tau$, integrations of functions (extra functions at a denominator cost), and a numerically optimised contour — and won by $0.005$.

## 4. Why Catalan does not work on its modular hosts — CDT's own sentence, and ours

CDT (Remark after Lemma 9.1.4): Zagier's row $\mathbf E$ has singularities $\{0,\tfrac18;\tfrac14,\infty\}$, the first two overconvergent, denominators $[1..n]^2$; since $e^2>16\cdot\tfrac14$, "this definitely precludes an approach to the irrationality of the Catalan constant by our method … unless some completely new idea is discovered." Zudilin's hypergeometric row is worse: denominators $16^n[1..2n]^2$, singularities $\varphi_{\rm golden}^{\pm5}$, $e^4>\varphi^5$ "by a wide margin".

What we have added, in order:
1. **The host is forced.** Catalan's Eisenstein class has weight $3$, so the row is a weight-$1$ row with $k=2$ (`WEIGHT_DROP.md`), it lives on the Beauville surface $I_8I_2I_1I_1$ (`HERFURTNER_CLASSIFICATION.md`), and every integral placement has characteristic roots that are integers with gaps $\ge1$ (`CUSP_MOVE_PROGRAM.md`, Theorem 4). So $|t_2|\le\tfrac14$ on every realisation on that surface; the entry deficit $\log16+\log\tfrac14-2=-0.61$ is a motivic invariant, not a choice.
2. **Level 16 is the only genus-zero cover** ($X_0(32)$ has genus 1) and its geometry is no better: same ceiling $\log(256\cdot\tfrac14)$, and worse contour (extra singularity at relative radius $\tfrac12$).
3. **The $p$-adic place.** The adelic bound $\tau_{\rm ad}=\tau-\sum_p\gamma_p$ is a correct theorem (`ADELIC_HOLONOMY.md` §2) and the pure orbit does have $2$-adic slope $2$. But the conditional function has $2$-adic slope $0$ in $x$ — necessarily: under $G=a/b$, $h_n=bb_n-aa_n\approx(b\xi_2-a)a_n$ and $\xi_2=\tfrac12\zeta_2(2)$ is **irrational (Calegari 2005)**, so $b\xi_2-a\ne0$ and $H$ is $2$-adically as large as $A$ — and slope $-2$ in the symmetrised coordinate because the descent is not integral (`ADELIC_HOLONOMY.md` §7). With measured slopes the entry test fails on both hosts. *Calegari's $2$-adic theorem is exactly what kills the $2$-adic route to the archimedean Catalan.* This is a clean and, I think, new observation: the $p$-adic avatar of the hypothesis is false, so it cannot help.
4. **Doubly-small functions** (period $0$, unconditional, small at both places) exist — two of them at level 16 — and are the right kind of object, but the Eisenstein space supplies two where a dozen are needed, and the structural cap of `ADELIC_HOLONOMY.md` §4.3 bounds how many can exist.
5. **Cusps count classes.** The number of conditional generators is $\le\#\text{cusps}-1$ (`CDT_NONCONGRUENCE.md` §10); Catalan's hosts have $4$ and $6$ cusps but only one Catalan *direction* (the $\chi_{-4}$ inner orientation), the others carrying $\zeta(2)$ and $0$.

So on modular hosts the method is blocked by a motivic invariant ($|t_2|=\tfrac14$) and the $2$-adic escape is blocked by a theorem. Reinvention has to change the host or the kind of function.

## 5. What the lattice method knows that the holonomy method has not been told

The two-row construction that gives worthiness $1-\varepsilon$ (`CATALAN_AUDIT.md`, Lean) uses two rows $(a^Z,b^Z)$ (Zudilin, hypergeometric, $16^n$-denominators, $2$-adic slope $8$) and $(a^N,b^N)$ (Nesterenko) and the fact
$$v_2\bigl(a^Z_nb^N_n-a^N_nb^Z_n\bigr)\ \ge\ 24n-O(\log n).$$
Read as functions: the coefficientwise expressions $a^Z_nb^N_n$ are **Hadamard products** $A_Z\odot B_N$. Hadamard products of $G$-functions are $G$-functions (CDT §9.1 use exactly this, "Hadamard products and Apéry limits"). Hence
$$W\ :=\ A_Z\odot B_N\ -\ A_N\odot B_Z\ \in\mathbb Q[[x]]$$
is an **unconditional** holonomic function with rational coefficients, denominator type (that of $b^Z$)$\cdot$(that of $b^N$), and $2$-adic slope $24$ in the coefficient — enormous compared with the slopes $0,1,2,3$ of everything on the modular hosts. Archimedean-ly, $W=A_Z\odot(B_N-GA_N)-A_N\odot(B_Z-GA_Z)$: both terms are Hadamard products of a full row with a *fold-regular* (decaying) linear form, so $|w_n|\ll(\lambda_1^Z\lambda_2^N)^n+(\lambda_1^N\lambda_2^Z)^n$, smaller than the naive $(\lambda_1^Z\lambda_1^N)^n$ by the decay factor: $W$ is overconvergent beyond the radius of $A_Z\odot A_N$. **$W$ is a doubly-small function in exactly the sense of `CATALAN_THREE_PERIOD.md`, built from two hosts instead of one, with a $2$-adic slope an order of magnitude larger.**

The lattice method uses $W$ only through $v_2(w_n)$ and the sizes of $a^Z_n,b^Z_n$; its obstruction (Lemma P2) is the nonvanishing of a single selected linear form. The holonomy method does not need nonvanishing; it needs a *dimension count* on a common host. The common host of $A_Z\odot B_N$ is the Hadamard host: singularities at the products $s_it_j$ of the singular points of the two rows (Hadamard's theorem), which is a $\mathbb P^1$ minus a finite set on which all of $A_Z\odot A_N$, $A_Z\odot B_N$, $A_N\odot B_Z$, $B_Z\odot B_N$, $W$, and the conditional function $b\,(A_Z\odot B_N)-a\,(A_Z\odot A_N)$ live.

**The design question is therefore finite:** on the Hadamard host of (Zudilin, Nesterenko) — or of (Zudilin, Zagier $\mathbf E$), or of the best adjacent pair $(j_0,j_0+1)$ found in `POSITIVITY_PROGRAM.md` — compute $\varphi$, $\tau$ (denominators are products, so $\tau$ is large: this is the cost), the $2$-adic slope profile ($W$: $24$; conditional: $-\kappa_Z-\kappa_N$, bad as always; pure Hadamard products of polylogs: whatever they are), and the margin with the adelic bound. The lattice design rule $\delta<1$ says the *single-form* version fails; the holonomy version asks a different question (can one bad conditional function be carried by unconditional functions of slope $24$?), and the rearrangement functional in `ADELIC_HOLONOMY.md` §2 is exactly the tool that answers it. I do not know the answer. It is the first Catalan computation in a month whose outcome I cannot predict from the structure, which is the criterion River asked for.

Two cautions. (i) Hadamard products square the number of singular points and the denominator exponents; $\tau$ may be so large that entry fails outright — the first thing to compute. (ii) The $\mathbb Q(x)$-independence of the Hadamard inventory must be checked, as always.

## 6. The computation

For the pair (Zudilin $(Q_m,P_m)$, Nesterenko $(V_n,U_n)$) at the aligned indices $(3n,n)$ used in `CATALAN_AUDIT.md`, and for the adjacent-pair family of `POSITIVITY_PROGRAM.md`:
1. Build the Hadamard functions $A_Z\odot A_N$, $A_Z\odot B_N$, $A_N\odot B_Z$, $B_Z\odot B_N$, $W$ as $t$-series to $n=400$; fit their minimal recurrences over $\mathbb Q$ (the singular set and the holonomic rank).
2. Measure: archimedean radii of each (and of $W$ — confirm the overconvergence), denominator types (exact $v_p$ profiles), $2$-adic slopes ($W$ should give $24-\kappa$'s).
3. Run the adelic holonomy bound on this host with the Koebe (univalent), Kodaira (multivalent) and, if an involution exists, symmetrised maps: entry and margin, for the inventory {pure Hadamard products of the polylog orbits}, {$W$ and its $\theta$-orbit}, {conditional generator and its orbit}.
4. Report in the per-function units of `CDT_NONCONGRUENCE.md`, alongside the modular hosts' $-0.61$ / $-6$.

## 7. What I now believe about "the clever idea"

The method has three slots: the host (conformal radius), the denominators ($\tau$), and the inventory ($m$). On modular hosts all three are motivic invariants of the Catalan class and are all unfavourable. The lattice method's $1-\varepsilon$ is evidence that the *two-host* object carries arithmetic the single-host functions do not. Whether that arithmetic survives the translation into a dimension count is the computation above. If it fails, the honest statement is: Catalan needs a host for the $\chi_{-4}$ class with $|t_2|>e^2/16$, and no such host exists in the elliptic-surface, non-congruence, cusp-move, or number-field worlds we have classified — so it needs a host outside them.
