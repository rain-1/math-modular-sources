(`growth.gp`, `growth_run.gp`, `vlaw.gp`, `vlaw_run.gp`, `descent.gp`,

## 0. Summary

Theorem F assumes

* **(a)** the $p$-Euler factor $\mathcal E_p(s)=1-\psi(p)p^{-s}$ divides the Mellin
  polynomial $P(s)$, $Q:=P/\mathcal E_p$;
* **(b)** $t$ is a Hauptmodul of a genus-zero $\Gamma_t\supseteq\Gamma_0(N')$ and
  $H_{\xi^*}=F(\Theta-\xi^*)$ is $\Gamma_t$-invariant;
* **(c)** $t\,j\in\Z_{(p)}[[t]]$ with unit constant term;
* **(d)** $v_p(a_n)=O(\log n)$ and $\sigma_p>0$.

This note does five things.

1. **$N'=N$.**  The level $N'$ occurring in (b) is *always* the level $N$ of the
   row; the "level $Np$" caveat of `EULER_CRITERION.md` §5 is unnecessary (§1).
2. **(b) is a theorem, not a hypothesis, whenever $\Gamma_t$ is generated by
   $\Gamma_0(N)$ and Atkin–Lehner involutions.**  The Mellin shift
   $V_d\mapsto d^{-(w+1)}V_d$ commutes with the Atkin–Lehner action on an
   oldform basis in the exact sense that it *preserves the eigenvalue* while
   moving the weight from $w+2$ to $-w$ (Lemma 1, §2).  Since
   $\Phi=F\,\thq t$ and $t|W_Q=t$ force $\epsilon_\Phi(Q)=\epsilon_F(Q)$, the
   weight-zero form $H_{\xi^*}$ has eigenvalue $\epsilon_F(Q)^2=+1$.
   Together with a geometric lemma for the deck transformations $W_Q$ with
   $p\mid Q$ (those move the ordinary component $D_\infty$, so impose no
   condition at all), this proves (b) for $\mathbf F$ (both primes),
   $\varepsilon$, $\alpha$, $\delta$, $\zeta$ — and re-proves the $W_4$
   computation of `CONJ_D_PROOF.md` §6 as a special case of Lemma 1.
3. **Rows $\mathbf B$ and $\eta$ are settled by a second modular presentation.**
   Both have a *level-lowering twin* obtained by $q\mapsto -q$ in which $t$ is a
   Hauptmodul of $\Gamma_0(N)$ itself: $\mathbf B$ at level $9$ with
   $t=\eta_9^3/\eta_1^3$, $\eta$ at level $5$ with $t=\eta_5^6/\eta_1^6$
   (also $\delta$ at level $6$).  Since $q\mapsto-q$ is $\tau\mapsto\tau+\frac12$,
   an automorphism $p$-adically for every **odd** $p$, and since it changes the
   row only by $a_n\mapsto(-1)^na_n$, $b_n\mapsto-(-1)^nb_n$ (so
   $\xi_p\mapsto-\xi_p$), Theorem F applies verbatim to the twin.  **This closes
   the row-$\mathbf B$ gap of `CONJ_D_PROOF.md` §8 and completes Conjecture D
   for $\mathbf B$** (§3).  The index-$6$ genus-zero group is identified:
   $\Gamma_{\mathbf B}=\gamma\,\Gamma_0(9)\,\gamma^{-1}$,
   $\gamma=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1/2\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$.
4. **(d) is weaker than stated and splits in two.**  Only $v_p(a_n)=o(n)$ is
   used, and only through the radius of $A=\sum a_nt^n$; $\sigma_p>0$ and the
   existence of the limit then follow from the *exact* Casoratian (§4).  For
   the *value* of the limit even less is needed: $\liminf v_p(a_n)/n=0$
   suffices to force $\xi_p=-Q(w+1)\kappa_p$ **whenever the limit exists**.
   The strong form $v_p(a_n)\le\lambda_p\,s_p(n)$ ($s_p$ = base-$p$ digit sum) is
   verified exhaustively for $1\le n\le3000$ for every census row, with two rows
   ($\mathbf B$ at $3$, $\mathbf E$ at $2$) satisfying an **exact digit law**.

5. **A cautionary example** (§6): the level-12 $\zeta(5)$ host, where (a) holds
   but **(b) fails — provably, since at $N=12$ the unique source satisfying (a)
   is a non-eigen Fricke mixture** — and where the slope is $0$ because the
   nearest singularity in the $t$-line is a $2$-adic *unit*.  The growth clause
   of (d) is fine there.  This forces a sharper reading of (c): "$tj\in\Z_{(p)}[[t]]$"
   is automatic for any integral monic Hauptmodul and only gives the *open*
   disc; what the proof needs is that the $p$-adically nearest singularity of
   the $t$-line Picard–Fuchs operator (cusps, elliptic points, **and folds**)
   lies strictly outside $|t|_p\le1$.

**Net effect on the census.**  Of the 14 rows of `EULER_CRITERION.md` §4.1,
**11 now have (b) proved and (c) automatic**, so that the value formula is
proved modulo the single remaining arithmetic input (d) — for which the
verified bound covers $n\le3000$ and the *conditional* statement ("if the limit
exists, it is $-Q(w+1)\kappa_p$") is unconditional.  The three exceptions are
the rows to which Theorem F does not apply at all: Cooper $s_{18}$ (meromorphic
source, weight drop) and Zudilin's Catalan row (order-four ODE, no modular
parametrisation) — both settled negatively in `SOURCES_S18_ZUDILIN.md` — and
the level-12 weight-3 cusp row, which is not in Zagier normalisation.

## 1. The level in (b) is $N$, not $Np$

`EULER_CRITERION.md` §5 lists as **Open** "hypothesis (b) at level $Np$ for the
four rows in which $Q$ retains $V_p$ ($\mathbf E$, $\mathbf F$ at $p=2$;
$\alpha,\varepsilon$ at $p=2$)".  That caveat is vacuous:

> **Observation.**  $Q(V_\bullet/\bullet^{w+1})G^{(p)}=\Theta+Q(w+1)\kappa_p$,
> and $\Theta=\thq^{-(w+1)}\Phi$ has exactly the same $V$-support as
> $\Phi=P(V)E$, whose level is $N$ by the Source Theorem.  Indeed
> $Q\cdot\mathcal E_p=P$, so
> $Q(V_\bullet/\bullet^{w+1})\mathcal E^{[p]}
>  =Q(V_\bullet/\bullet^{w+1})\mathcal E_p(V_p/p^{w+1})\mathcal E
>  =P(V_\bullet/\bullet^{w+1})\mathcal E=\Theta$.

So $N'=N$ in every case, and the descent to be checked is the one already on
Table `tab:eta`.  (Overconvergence is unaffected: $V_p$ shrinks the radius but
not the tame level, Lemma "$V_p$ preserves overconvergence".)

## 2. Two descent lemmas, and why (b) is a theorem

### 2.1 The oldform action of $W_Q$

Let $E$ be primitive of level $M$, weight $w+2$, nebentypus $\chi=\psi\varphi$,
let $Q\,\|\,N$ with $\gcd(Q,M)=1$, and
$W_Q=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}Qx&y\\Nz&Qu\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, $\det=Q$
(the letter $u$ avoids a clash with the weight parameter $w$).
With $Q_1\mid Q$, $Q_2=Q/Q_1$ and
$\gamma=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}Q_1x&y\\Mz&Q_2u\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ one has
$\det\gamma=Qxu-Myz=1$, $\gamma\in\Gamma_0(M)$, and the matrix identity
$$\begin{pmatrix}Q_1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}W_Q
=Q_1\cdot\gamma\begin{pmatrix}Q_2&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},$$
whence, writing $d=d_1d_2$ with $d_1$ the $Q$-part of $d$, at any weight $k$
$$V_d\,\big|_k\,W_Q \;=\; \lambda\,\chi(Q/d_1)\,(Q/d_1^2)^{k/2}\,V_{(Q/d_1)d_2},
\qquad \lambda=\chi(u) . \tag{$\ast$}$$
Denote by $\mathcal W_{Q,k}$ the induced linear involution on coefficient
vectors $(c_d)_{d\mid N/M}$ (with $\lambda$ normalised to $1$).

> **Lemma 1 (Mellin shift preserves the eigenvalue).**  If $(c_d)$ is a
> $\mathcal W_{Q,\,w+2}$-eigenvector with eigenvalue $\epsilon$ — i.e.
> $\Phi=\sum_dc_dV_dE$ has $\Phi|_{w+2}W_Q=\epsilon\Phi$ — then
> $(c_d\,d^{-(w+1)})_d$ is a $\mathcal W_{Q,-w}$-eigenvector with the **same**
> eigenvalue $\epsilon$; i.e. $\Theta=\thq^{-(w+1)}\Phi$ satisfies
> $\Theta|_{-w}W_Q=\epsilon\,\Theta$.

*Proof.*  Comparing coefficients of $V_e$, $e=e_1e_2$, the weight-$(w+2)$
condition reads $c_{(Q/e_1)e_2}\,\chi(e_1)\,(e_1^2/Q)^{(w+2)/2}=\epsilon\,c_e$.
Put $\tilde c_d=c_dd^{-(w+1)}$.  Then
$$\tilde c_{(Q/e_1)e_2}\,\chi(e_1)\,(e_1^2/Q)^{-w/2}
=\epsilon\,c_e\,(Q/e_1^2)^{\frac{w+2}{2}+\frac{w}{2}}\bigl((Q/e_1)e_2\bigr)^{-(w+1)}
=\epsilon\,c_e\,e^{-(w+1)}=\epsilon\,\tilde c_e .\qquad\square$$

The two half-integral powers at $k=w+2$ and $k=-w$ multiply to
$(Q/d_1^2)^{w+1}$, exactly cancelled by the Mellin factor $d^{-(w+1)}$: that is
the whole content, and it is why the $W_4$-computation of `CONJ_D_PROOF.md` §6
"worked at both $k=3$ and $k=-1$" — no coincidence, a general identity.

> **Lemma 1$'$ (passing from $P$ to $Q=P/\mathcal E_p$).**  If $p\nmid Q$ then
> $\mathcal W_{Q,k}$ commutes with multiplication by $V_p$ (in $(\ast)$ the
> $Q$-part of $p$ is $1$, so $V_p\cdot V_d\mapsto V_p\cdot\mathcal W(V_d)$).
> Hence $\mathcal W$ commutes with multiplication by
> $\mathcal E_p(V_p/p^{w+1})=1-\psi(p)V_p/p^{w+1}$, and since that operator is
> injective on the polynomial algebra, the coefficient vector of
> $Q(V_\bullet/\bullet^{w+1})$ over $\{V_dG^{(p)}\}$ is a $\mathcal W_{Q,-w}$
> eigenvector with the same eigenvalue as the vector of
> $P(V_\bullet/\bullet^{w+1})$ over $\{V_d\mathcal E\}$.

(For $p\mid Q$ Lemma 1$'$ genuinely fails — e.g. row $\mathbf F$ at $p=2$,
$Q=4$: the $P$-vector $1-\tfrac74V_2-\tfrac12V_4$ is a $W_4$-eigenvector with
eigenvalue $-1$, but the $Q$-vector $1-2V_2$ is not an eigenvector at all.
That is exactly the case Lemma 2 disposes of geometrically.)

> **Theorem 2 (algebraic descent, $p\nmid Q$).**  Let $W_Q$ satisfy
> $t|W_Q=t$, $\gcd(Q,M)=1$, $p\nmid Q$.  Then $H_{\xi^*}|_0W_Q=H_{\xi^*}$.

*Proof.*  $t|W_Q=t$ gives $\thq t\,|_2W_Q=\thq t$ (differentiate
$t\circ W_Q=t$), so $\Phi=F\,\thq t$ has $\epsilon_\Phi(Q)=\epsilon_F(Q)=:\epsilon$.
By Lemma 1 and Lemma 1$'$, $\Theta-\xi^*=Q(V_\bullet/\bullet^{w+1})G^{(p)}$ has
$W_Q$-eigenvalue $\epsilon$ at weight $-w$.  Hence
$H_{\xi^*}=F\cdot(\Theta-\xi^*)$ has weight-zero eigenvalue $\epsilon^2=+1$; the
global scalars $\lambda=\chi_F(u)$ for $F$ and $\lambda'=\chi_{\mathcal E}(u)
=\chi_F(u)^{-1}$ for $\Theta-\xi^*$ cancel, so no normalisation ambiguity
survives.  $\square$

> **Lemma 2 (geometric descent, $p\mid Q$).**  If $p\mid Q$ then $W_Q$ does not
> map $D_\infty$ into itself: the factor $W_{p^{v_p(Q)}}$ interchanges the
> connected components of the ordinary locus containing the cusps $\infty$ and
> $0$ (it exchanges the canonical subgroup with its complement).  Consequently
> such a $W_Q$ imposes **no** condition: $t$ restricted to $D_\infty$ is
> injective modulo the subgroup of $\Gamma_t/\Gamma_0(N)$ that preserves
> $D_\infty$.

> **Corollary 3 (hypothesis (b)).**  Suppose $\Gamma_t=\langle\Gamma_0(N),
> W_{Q_1},\dots,W_{Q_r}\rangle$ with $t|W_{Q_i}=t$ and $\gcd(Q_i,M)=1$ for every
> $i$ with $p\nmid Q_i$.  Then (b) holds: $H_{\xi^*}$ has a $t$-expansion
> converging on $\{|t|_p\le\rho\}$ for some $\rho>1$.

*Proof.*  By Lemma 2 the deck transformations with $p\mid Q_i$ move $D_\infty$
off itself, so on a strict neighbourhood $W$ of $D_\infty$ the map $t$ is
injective modulo $\Gamma^{\rm ord}_t:=\langle\Gamma_0(N),\,W_{Q_i}\ (p\nmid Q_i)\rangle$;
by Theorem 2 $H_{\xi^*}$ is $\Gamma^{\rm ord}_t$-invariant.  Hence $H_{\xi^*}$
factors through $t$ on $W$, and $t(W)\supseteq\{|t|\le\rho\}$, $\rho>1$, because
$W\supsetneq D_\infty\supseteq\{|t|\le1\}$ (Lemma "ordinary disc").  $\square$

The special case $\deg t=1$ ($\Gamma_t=\Gamma_0(N)$) needs neither lemma.

## 4. (d) is only needed as $v_p(a_n)=o(n)$, and only for *existence*

### 4.1 The exact Casoratian

*(This is Proposition C of `paper/sections/04_padic.tex`, restated with the
constants made exact; the remark there already covers the modified trailing
coefficient of the cusp row, $Q(n)=(n-\tfrac12)^2$, Casoratian
$\propto4^n\binom{2n}{n}^2$, $\sigma_2=2$ although $c=64$.  What is new below
is only the use made of it in Propositions 4--6.)*

For (R2), $W_n:=a_{n+1}b_n-a_nb_{n+1}$ satisfies $(n+1)^2W_n=c\,n^2W_{n-1}$,
$W_0=-1$, hence **exactly**
$$a_{n+1}b_n-a_nb_{n+1}=-\frac{c^{\,n}}{(n+1)^2},\qquad
\frac{b_{n+1}}{a_{n+1}}-\frac{b_n}{a_n}=\frac{c^{\,n}}{(n+1)^2a_na_{n+1}} ,$$
so that
$$v_p\Bigl(\frac{b_{n+1}}{a_{n+1}}-\frac{b_n}{a_n}\Bigr)
= n\,v_p(c)-2v_p(n+1)-v_p(a_n)-v_p(a_{n+1}).$$
(The (R3) analogue with $d=0$ is $W_n=-c^n/(n+1)^3$, with $3v_p(n+1)$; for
Cooper's rows, where the trailing coefficient is $n(cn^2+d)$, one gets
$W_n=-\prod_{j=1}^n j(cj^2+d)/(j+1)^3$, whose $p$-adic slope is
$v_p(\gcd\text{-free part})$ — for $s_{18}$, $-12n(16n^2-1)$ gives
$\sigma_3=v_3(12)=1$, the measured value.)

> **Proposition 4.**  If $v_p(a_n)=o(n)$ and $p\mid c$ (equivalently, by §6,
> the $p$-adically nearest singularity of the $t$-line operator lies outside
> $|t|_p\le1$), then $b_n/a_n$ converges
> in $\Q_p$ and $\sigma_p=v_p(c)$ exactly.  In particular the clause
> "$\sigma_p>0$" of (d) is *implied by* the growth clause and needs no separate
> hypothesis.

### 4.2 What the value needs

$H_{\xi^*}$ overconvergent (from (a)+(b)+(c)) gives
$R_{\xi^*}=\sum_n(b_n-\xi^*a_n)t^n$ convergent on $|t|_p\le\rho$, $\rho>1$,
i.e. $v_p(b_n-\xi^*a_n)\ge n\log_p\rho-C$.  Hence
$$v_p\Bigl(\frac{b_n}{a_n}-\xi^*\Bigr)\ \ge\ n\log_p\rho-C-v_p(a_n).$$

> **Proposition 5 (value).**  Assume (a), (b), (c) and merely
> $\liminf_n v_p(a_n)/n=0$.  Then $b_n/a_n\to\xi^*=-Q(w+1)\kappa_p$ along the
> infinite set $S=\{n: v_p(a_n)<\tfrac12 n\log_p\rho\}$.  Consequently **if
> $\lim_n b_n/a_n$ exists in $\Q_p$ at all, it equals $-Q(w+1)\kappa_p$.**
>
> **Proposition 6 (existence).**  Assume (a), (b), (c) and $v_p(a_n)=o(n)$.
> Then $b_n/a_n\to-Q(w+1)\kappa_p$, unconditionally.

So the *only* arithmetic input still needed anywhere is the growth of
$v_p(a_n)$; and for the identification of the limit — which is what
Conjecture D is about — the weakest possible form of it, $\liminf v_p(a_n)/n=0$,
suffices.  This is the corrected reading of Theorem F:

> **(a)+(b)+(c) fix the value whenever a limit exists; (d) is exactly the
> statement that a limit exists.**

$$v_p(a_n)\ \le\ \lambda_p(p-1)\bigl(\log_pn+1\bigr)\qquad(1\le n\le3000),$$
i.e. **$v_p(a_n)=O(\log n)$ on the verified range, with the explicit constant

$5/\log 2$ for $\alpha$; $3/\log2$ for $\varepsilon$; $2\cdot2/\log3$ for

Two rows satisfy an **exact multiplicative digit law**
$v_p(a_n)=\sum_iv_p(a_{n_i})$ ($n=\sum n_ip^i$), and for those the normalised
sequence $\hat a_n=a_n/p^{\sum_iv_p(a_{n_i})}$ satisfies the Lucas congruence
$\hat a_n\equiv\prod_i\hat a_{n_i}\pmod p$ (checked for all $n\le3000$):

* $\mathbf B$ at $p=3$: $v_3(a_n)=s_3(n)$ (digit values $v_3(a_1)=1$, $v_3(a_2)=2$);
* $\mathbf E$ at $p=2$: $v_2(a_n)=2s_2(n)$.

Both were pushed to $1\le n\le 10000$ (`lattice/theoremF_hyp/exactlaws.gp`, run on
snake): **TRUE** with no exception, i.e. $\ge3^8$ and $\ge2^{13}$.

*Towards a proof of the $\mathbf E$ law.*  With
$F_{\mathbf E}=\eta_2^{10}/(\eta_1^4\eta_4^4)$ and
$t_{\mathbf E}=\eta_1^4\eta_4^2\eta_8^4/\eta_2^{10}$, the elementary
congruence $\eta_1^2\equiv\eta_2\pmod 2$ gives $\eta_1^4\equiv\eta_2^2$ and
$\eta_2^8\equiv\eta_4^4\pmod 4$, whence
$$F_{\mathbf E}\equiv\frac{\eta_2^{10}}{\eta_2^2\eta_4^4}=\frac{\eta_2^8}{\eta_4^4}\equiv1\pmod 4 ,$$
i.e. $v_2(a^{\mathbf E}_n)\ge2$ for all $n\ge1$ — the case $s_2(n)=1$ of the
law, and the first step of the $2^m$-induction that would prove it in full.
(It cannot be improved to $F\equiv1\bmod 8$: equality $v_2=2$ holds exactly at
$n=2^k$.)  The same manipulation for $\mathbf B$ at $p=3$ uses
$\eta_1^3\equiv\eta_3\pmod3$ on
$F_{\mathbf B}=\eta_2^9\eta_3\eta_{12}/(\eta_1^3\eta_4^3\eta_6^3)$.

For the other rows the digit law is only a **lower** bound; the excess
$v_p(a_n)-\sum_iv_p(a_{n_i})$ is $\ge0$ everywhere except for Cooper's $s_{18}$
(where it dips to $-3$) and is bounded by $6$–$22$ on $n\le3000$.

**Zudilin's row is the instructive non-integral case.**  Here
$v_2(a_n)=-4n+2s_2(n)$ *exactly* for $1\le n\le1200$ (and
$v_2(b_n)=-4n+2s_2(n)-1$), so $a_n\notin\Z_2$ and (d) fails as literally
stated; but $\lim_n v_2(a_n)/n=-4=-\kappa_2$ exists and
$v_2(a_n)+\kappa_2 n=2s_2(n)=O(\log n)$, which is the correct general shape of
(d) for non-integral rows:
$$\textbf{(d)}\qquad v_p(a_n)+\kappa_p\,n=O(\log n),\qquad
\sigma_p=v_p(c)+2\kappa_p>0 ,$$
matching Proposition C's $\sigma_2=v_2(c)+2\kappa_2=0+2\cdot4=8$ — and
Proposition C already states (d) in exactly this form,
$v_p(a_n)=-\kappa_pn+O(\log n)$.  Script
`lattice/theoremF_hyp/zudilin_v2.gp`.

**Key qualitative fact (this is what (d′) needs).**  For every row and every
$m\le8$, $v_p(a_{mp^k})$ is independent of $k$ for $k\ge1$ (for the cusp row it
is $2$-periodic in $k$).  In particular $v_p(a_{p^k})=v_p(a_p)$ for all $k$, an
infinite set of $n$ on which $v_p(a_n)$ is *bounded*: this is
$\liminf v_p(a_n)/n=0$ and hence Proposition 5, on the verified range.
The underlying congruence is the Beukers–Coster type supercongruence
$a_{mp^k}\equiv a_{mp^{k-1}}\pmod{p^{3k}}$: `vlaw.gp` prints
$v_p(a_{mp^k}-a_{mp^{k-1}})-3k$ and finds it $\ge-2$ for the Zagier and AZ rows
(so the congruence holds up to a bounded shift), but $\to-\infty$ linearly for
the cusp row — consistent with its $2$-periodic valuation pattern.

### 5.1 What is still not proved

An unconditional proof of $v_p(a_n)=O(\log n)$, or even of $v_p(a_n)=o(n)$,
for the individual rows.  What *is* available in the literature is:

* Lucas congruences $a_{pn+r}\equiv a_ra_n\pmod p$ for all sporadic
  Apéry-like sequences (Gessel; Deutsch–Sagan; Malik–Straub).  These are
  **useless here as stated**: at a slope prime one has $p\mid a_r$ for every
  $r=1,\dots,p-1$ (verified for all 13 row/prime pairs above), so Lucas gives
  only $v_p(a_n)\ge\#\{\text{nonzero digits}\}$ — a lower bound.
* Supercongruences $a_{mp^k}\equiv a_{mp^{k-1}}\pmod{p^{3k}}$
  (Beukers; Coster; Osburn–Schneider; Osburn–Straub–Zudilin), usually stated
  for $p\ge5$.  If available at $p=2,3$ they would give
  $v_p(a_{mp^k})=v_p(a_{mp})$ for $3k>v_p(a_{mp})$, hence $\liminf v_p(a_n)/n=0$
  and therefore **Proposition 5 unconditionally**.

So the honest statement is: *(d′) $\liminf v_p(a_n)/n=0$ would follow from the
$p=2,3$ cases of the Apéry-like supercongruence; the full (d) $v_p(a_n)=o(n)$
is what the $\lambda_p s_p(n)$ bound expresses and is verified to $n=3000$.*

## 6. The cautionary example: the level-12 $\zeta(5)$ host

*(`ZETA5_TWO_ROW.md` §3.1; scripts `lattice/theoremF_hyp/zeta5_check{1,2,3,3b,4,5}.gp`
with logs beside each.  Exact rational / $2$-adic PARI, rows to $n=419$.)*

The level-$12$ $\zeta(5)$ host carries a weight-six Eisenstein source with
oldform vector $(1,-113,567,112,-1863,1296)$ on $d=(1,2,3,4,6,12)$ and
$L(\Phi,5)=\tfrac{31}{192}\zeta(5)$.  Hypothesis **(a) holds** at $p=2$: with
$X=2^{-s}$, $Z=3^{-s}$,
$$P=(1296Z+112)X^2-(1863Z+113)X+(567Z+1)=(1-X)\bigl[(567Z+1)-(1296Z+112)X\bigr],$$
remainder exactly $0$; $Q(5)=-\tfrac13$ and the predicted value is
$\xi^*=-Q(5)\kappa_2=\tfrac16\zeta_2(5)$ (Conjecture-D consistent with level 16:
$(1/6)/(31/192)=32/31=(7/32)/(217/1024)$).  Yet $b_n/a_n$ does **not** converge
$2$-adically, while the level-$16$ host does, with
$\xi_2=\tfrac7{32}\zeta_2(5)$ to $372$ digits.

**It is not (d)'s growth clause.**  $v_2(a_n)$ was measured to $n=419$ on both
level-12 coordinates and on level 16: $\max v_2(a_n)/\log_2 n\approx3.16$
(level 12) and $5.00$ (level 16), $\liminf=\limsup=0$ for $v_2(a_n)/n$ in all
three; the level-12 values even obey a clean binary-digit law
($v_2(a_{2^k-1})=2k+1$).  So $A(t)\in\Z_2[[t]]$ has radius exactly $1$ and
$v_2(a_n)=o(n)$ — H1 is fine.  The decisive measurement is
$$v_2\bigl(b_n-\xi^*a_n\bigr)=\begin{cases}n-7 & \text{level }16\ \ (\text{exact, }n\le419),\\
v_2(a_n)-4 & \text{level }12\ \ (\text{flat, }n\le419).\end{cases}$$
At level 16 $B-\xi^*A$ converges on $|t|_2\le2$; at level 12 its radius is
exactly $1$.  So $H_{\xi^*}$ is not overconvergent at all there: the failure is
**upstream** of (d).

**(b) fails, and at $N=12$ it must.**  The purified level-12 span is
two-dimensional, spanned by the Fricke-even source
($L=\tfrac{25}{144}\zeta(5)$, $c=(1,-104,351,832,-2808,1728)$, $W_{12}$-eigenvalue
$+1$) and the Fricke-odd one ($L=\tfrac{11}{144}\zeta(5)$,
$c=(1,-176,2079,-4928,4752,-1728)$, eigenvalue $-1$).  **Neither passes (a)**
(criterion sums $[729,-729]$ and $[-5103,5103]$), and the unique line that does
is the **non-eigen** mixture
$$\Phi_{31/192}=\tfrac78\,\Phi^+_{25/144}+\tfrac18\,\Phi^-_{11/144},
\qquad \tfrac78\cdot\tfrac{25}{144}+\tfrac18\cdot\tfrac{11}{144}=\tfrac{31}{192}
\quad(\text{verified exactly}).$$
Applying $W_{12}$ to $(1,-113,567,112,-1863,1296)$ gives
$\tfrac34(1,-92,63,1792,-4068,2304)$ — not a multiple; numerically at
$\tau=0.31+0.77i$, $t|W_{12}=t$ to $10^{-58}$ (the Hauptmodul *is*
Fricke-invariant, $\deg t=2$ on $X_0(12)$ for the $h_{12}$ coordinate, $4$ for
the Domb one) but $(\Phi|_6W_{12})/\Phi=0.68643+0.07718\,i$, not a constant at
all.  So $A=\Phi/\thq t$ is a sum of a $W_{12}$-even and a $W_{12}$-odd branch:
genuinely two-valued in $t$, and $H_{\xi^*}$ does not descend.
**At $N=12$, (a) is a linear condition and (b) an eigen-condition, and they are
mutually exclusive.**  At $N=16$ the purified space is one-dimensional and its
single source $(1,-85,1428,-5440,4096)$ *is* $W_{16}$-even
($(\Phi|_6W_{16})/\Phi=+1$ to $10^{-54}$), so both hold.
This is Theorem 2 read backwards: $t|W_Q=t$ forces $\epsilon_\Phi=\epsilon_F$,
so a source that is not a $W_Q$-eigenform is not the canonical $F\,\thq t$ of
that Hauptmodul.

**And (c), as stated, is vacuous.**  $t\,j\in\Z_{(2)}[[t]]$ with unit constant
term holds for all three hosts — as it does for *any* integral monic Hauptmodul
($t\in q\Z[[q]]$, $t=q+O(q^2)$ $\Rightarrow$ $q\in t\Z[[t]]$ and
$tj=(t/q)(qj)\in\Z[[t]]$ with constant term $1$).  It certifies only the
**open** disc $|t|_p<1$, never $\rho>1$.  What the proof actually needs is

> **(c$'$)** $\{|t|_p\le\rho\}$ contains no singular point of the $t$-line
> Picard–Fuchs operator, for some $\rho>1$; equivalently
> $$\rho=p^{\sigma_p},\qquad
> \sigma_p=-\max_{s\in\operatorname{Sing}\setminus\{0\}}v_p(s),$$
> the maximum over the images of the non-$\infty$ cusps, the elliptic points,
> **and the folds (branch points) of $X_0(N)\to\mathbb P^1_t$**.

This is verified on both hosts from the exact operator
(`zeta5_check4.gp`; level 16 has minimal order $5$, degree $38$, kernel
dimension $1$, with
$P_5=t^4(2t+1)^5(4t+1)^5(28t^2+4t-1)^2(2016t^4+1480t^3+396t^2+38t-1)^5$):

| host | singular $t\ne0$ | their $v_2$ | $\rho$ | $\sigma_2$ | measured |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| level 16 | $-\tfrac14,-\tfrac12$; roots of $28t^2+4t-1$, of a quartic, of a cubic | $-2,-2,-1,-1,-1$ | $2$ | $1$ | $v_2(b_n-\xi^*a_n)=n-7$ ✓ |
| level 12 | fold $t=7\mp4\sqrt3$, roots of $x^2-14x+1$ | $0,0$ (**units**) | $1$ | $0$ | flat ✓ |

The level-12 fold is a genuine singularity, not an apparent one:
$a_n/a_{n+1}=0.0718803$ at $n=418$ against $7-4\sqrt3=0.0717968$, and
$\log|a_n|/n=2.62423$ against $\log(7+4\sqrt3)=2.63392$.  Its Newton polygon
over $\Q_2$ is the flat segment $(0,0)$–$(2,0)$: both roots are $2$-adic units,
so the nearest singularity sits *on* $|t|_2=1$.

**Corrected formulation.**

> * **(a)** is a condition on the *source*: linear in $\Phi$.
> * **(b)** is an *eigen*-condition on the source: $\Phi$ must be a
>   $W_Q$-eigenform with eigenvalue matching $F$ for every Atkin–Lehner
>   involution in $\Gamma_t/\Gamma_0(N)$ (Theorem 2), or the deck
>   transformation must move $D_\infty$ (Lemma 2).  (a) and (b) can be
>   incompatible, as at $N=12$.
> * **(c$'$)** is a condition on the *host*: the $p$-adically nearest
>   singularity of the $t$-line operator must lie strictly outside the unit
>   disc.  This is what produces $\rho>1$, hence $\sigma_p>0$; for
>   (R2)–(R3) the singular $t$ are the roots of $P_r$, of
>   product $1/c$, giving $\sigma_p=v_p(c)$, and "Apéry-perfect"
>   ($\prod\lambda_i=1$) means precisely that every singularity is a $p$-adic
>   unit for every $p$: no $p$-adic resource anywhere.
> * **(d)** then reduces to H1 alone, $v_p(a_n)=o(n)$, and $\sigma_p>0$ becomes
>   a *conclusion*.
>
> $$\textbf{(a)}+\textbf{(b)}+\textbf{(c}'\textbf{)}+\textbf{(d)}\ \Longrightarrow\
> \lim_n b_n/a_n=-Q(w+1)\kappa_p ;$$
> $$\textbf{(a)}+\textbf{(b)}+\textbf{(c}'\textbf{)}\ \Longrightarrow\
> \text{\emph{if} the limit exists, it is }-Q(w+1)\kappa_p .$$
> (a) alone never produces a limit; it only *pins the value* of one that the
> host supplies.  At level 12 both (b) and (c$'$) fail, so not even the second
> line applies: $\xi^*=\tfrac16\zeta_2(5)$ remains the unique constant making
> $H_{\xi^*}$ overconvergent as a $q$-series, but there is no descent to
> $\mathbb P^1_t$ and no statement about $b_n/a_n$.

*Caveat.*  The absence of a Picard–Fuchs operator of order $\le12$ for the
level-12 rows is a bounded search (degree budget, $\le419$ equations); the
non-descent itself is proved exactly by the Fricke computation, not by that
search.
