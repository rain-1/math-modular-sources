# Sabba's alternating continued fractions of the integers: structure, and why they are not E-function values

*Fable, 2026-09-02. Prompted by M. Sabba's thread on the Rabinowitz/Amoretti/Fourier continued fractions and the "Sabba constants". Agent report and scripts in `lattice/sabba_constants/` (`REPORT.md`, `summary.py` regenerates every number). Nothing here touches the modular Apéry programme; it is a closure note on a side question: is the $n!$-world (E-functions, Siegel–Shidlovsky) a route worth developing in parallel to the $\mathrm{lcm}(1..n)$-world of this repository?*

## 1. The four constants

$$R=1+\cfrac1{2+\cfrac1{3+\cdots}}=\frac{I_0(2)}{I_1(2)},\qquad
F=1-\cfrac1{2-\cfrac1{3-\cdots}}=\frac{J_0(2)}{J_1(2)}$$
(Euler 1737; Fourier 1822; Rabinowitz 1990/Robbins 1995; the arithmetic-progression version is Lehmer, *Scripta Math.* 29 (1973)), and Sabba's two period-2 alternating versions
$$C=\cfrac1{1+\cfrac1{2-\cfrac1{3+\cfrac1{4-\ddots}}}}=0.62873660709895479943558790225263029538343274690833\ldots,\qquad
D=1-\cfrac1{2+\cfrac1{3-\cfrac1{4+\ddots}}}=0.57663338973018439239789174978291392579614943527571\ldots$$
(OEIS A244279/A244280, A262957/A263295). All four come from one recurrence, $y_n=n\,y_{n-1}+\varepsilon_ny_{n-2}$, with $\varepsilon_n\equiv\pm1$ for $R,F$ and $\varepsilon_n=a(-1)^n$, $a=\pm1$, for $C,D$. By Pincherle the value is a ratio of consecutive terms of the *minimal* solution: $C=1/(1-r_{+1})$, $D=1-r_{-1}$ with $r_a=y_1/y_0$ [proved].

**Known Diophantine facts.** Peter Bala (note attached to A262957, 2015) proves, from the identity $a_1-\frac1{a_2+x}=a_1-1+\frac1{1+\frac1{a_2-1+x}}$, that the simple continued fractions are $C=[0;1,1,1,2,3,1,4,5,1,6,7,\dots]$ and $D=[0;1,1,2,1,3,4,1,5,6,\dots]$; hence both are irrational with irrationality measure exactly $2$ (optimal). Transcendence is known for $R$ and $F$ (Siegel 1929: values of Bessel E-functions at algebraic points) and is **open** for $C$ and $D$.

**OEIS erratum.** The comments in A244279/A244280 quote $C=0.628736607098954801603428\ldots$, which is wrong from the 16th decimal; the correct digits are $\ldots0989547994355879\ldots$ (three independent computations here: the convergents, the minimal solution by backward recurrence, and the fact that our value reproduces Bala's proved pattern through 40 partial quotients while the quoted decimal breaks it at the 18th).

## 2. Structure [proved]

1. *EGF.* $f_a(t)=\sum y_nt^n/n!$ satisfies the reflection equation $((1-t)f)''=a\,f(-t)$, hence the rank-4 operator $(1-t^2)f''''-(6t+2)f'''-6f''-f=0$; for $F=(1-t)f$ and $x=(1+t)/2$ this is exactly the operator posted in the thread, $x(1-x)F''''+2(1-x)F'''-4F=0$. Regular singularities at $x=0$ (exponents $0,1,1,2$) and $x=1$ (exponents $0,1,2,3$, with a $c_0X^3\log X$ obstruction), irregular of slope $1/2$ at $\infty$.
2. *The posted "closed form" is a theorem.* With $P_a,Q_a$ normalised at $x=1/2$ as in the thread, $C=2Q_{+1}(1^-)/P_{+1}(1^-)$ and $D=P_{-1}(1^-)/(2Q_{-1}(1^-))$: the data $(1,0,4a,-8a)$, $(0,1,0,-4a)$ are $(F,F',F'',F''')(\tfrac12)$ for $(y_0,y_1)=(1,1)$ and $(0,\tfrac12)$, and $F(1^-)=0$ is precisely the condition that $f_a$ be entire, i.e. that $y$ be the minimal solution. Verified to 53 digits. It is a connection-coefficient identity, the same kind of statement as "the Apéry limit is the value of the Eichler integral at the fold", not a closed form.
3. *Laplace dual.* The ordinary generating function $Y=\sum y_nz^n$ (entire, exponential type 1) satisfies, with $W=zY$,
$$W''=\Bigl(\frac1{z^4}-\frac2{z^3}-a^2\Bigr)W,$$
a **doubly-confluent Heun equation**: rank-1 irregular singularities at $0$ and $\infty$ and no other singular point; equivalently, after $W=e^{-1/z}V$, $V''+\frac2{z^2}V'+V=0$. This is the Fourier–Laplace transform of the rank-4 EGF operator. The family $y_n=ny_{n-1}+\mu(-1)^ny_{n-2}$ gives $W''=(z^{-4}-2z^{-3}-\mu^2)W$: a one-parameter deformation of the solvable point $\mu=0$, with $\mu^2=1$ our case (the structural analogue of Mathieu).
4. *No classical closed form.* Kovacic's algorithm: no Liouvillian solutions, differential Galois group $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf C)$. The projective connection $Q\,dz^2$ has two poles of order 4 (Bessel/Whittaker/Kummer have one of order 4 and one of order 2), and pole orders are Möbius invariants; degree-$\ge2$ pullbacks are excluded by the rank count. So $C,D$ are not expressible through Bessel-type functions by any change of variable and gauge. All PSLQ searches (Kelvin functions at $2$, $I,J$ at $2$ and $2\sqrt2$, $\Gamma(1/4),\pi,e$, algebraicity to degree 8, and the guesses of the first pass) are negative.
5. *Not E-functions.* An E-operator is the Fourier–Laplace transform of a $G$-operator, in particular Fuchsian; the dual here is irregular at both $0$ and $\infty$, and irreducible. So no solution of the EGF operator is an E-function (elementary version: the entire solutions have transcendental Taylor coefficients, the algebraic-coefficient solutions have radius of convergence exactly 1). **Siegel–Shidlovsky and Beukers' 2006 refinement do not apply** to $C$ and $D$. They are exponential periods (connection/Stokes constants of an irreducible rank-2 irregular connection); a transcendence proof would need a theory of Stokes data of doubly-confluent Heun equations that does not exist.
6. *Two exact identities* (120 digits): $f_a(-1)=-2a\,f_a'(1)$, and the bilinear invariant coupling the two constants, $r_{+1}+r_{-1}-2=-(f_{+}(1)f_{-}(-1)+f_{-}(1)f_{+}(-1))$.

## 3. Verdict on the "$n!$-world" as a parallel programme

* For the non-alternating fractions the $n!$-world is the E-function world, where the Diophantine theory (Siegel–Shidlovsky, André's E-operators, Beukers) is already **stronger** than anything a holonomy count gives: values are exactly as transcendental as the functions. A CDT-style count with $n!$ denominators would be a weak re-derivation of Siegel's 1929 method (denominators $n!$ match the entire-function growth exactly, the disc becomes the plane, and the count degenerates into Siegel's lemma plus Shidlovsky's zero estimate). Not a route to develop.
* Sabba's constants show the other side: as soon as the recurrence is twisted, the objects leave the E-function world entirely (irreducible doubly-confluent Heun, $\mathrm{SL}_2$), and *no* current theory reaches their transcendence, although irrationality and the optimal measure $2$ are elementary. So the twisted $n!$-world is arithmetically *harder* than the modular $\mathrm{lcm}$-world, not a shortcut.
* The genuine bridge between the two worlds is the Fourier–Laplace transform, and its interesting instance for this repository is Broadhurst's Bessel moments (exponential periods equal to $L$-values of the same modular forms that host our Apéry systems), not continued fractions of Bessel ratios.
