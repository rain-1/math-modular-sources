The caveats you must state (and Zudilin will ask about):
1. It is a construction quality, not an irrationality measure. The bare statement "∃ admissible $(q,p)$ with $\delta>1-\varepsilon$" is true for every real number (convergents give $\delta\ge2$ for any irrational). The content is entirely that the sequence comes from an explicit, $G$-independent recipe with proved rates $H,F$ — the same notion of "quality" as in your papers, except the final selection step is non-constructive (Minkowski on the congruence lattice, as in Siegel's-lemma constructions), so $q_n,p_n$ are not given by a formula.
2. It is a family, not a sequence. $\delta(k)=D/(D+F(k))$ with $F(k)=\tfrac{\log2}2(k^-k)>0$; each member has $\delta<1$; no single sequence reaches 1. At $k=k^*$ the argument degenerates to Dirichlet's theorem, and we can show (our §6) that crossing 1 by this route is *equivalent* to irrationality — the "$\delta>1$" numerics one can produce past $k^$ are reproduced verbatim by a rational number with a $10^{320}$-digit denominator. So: no irrationality, and no honest path to it from this construction alone.
3. Calibration: in this metric your own row gives $\delta=0.5265$, the LCM-only combination with Nesterenko $0.6589$ (also proved), and the earlier "0.9025" (your row × the level-8 modular row at 5:8) rests on a 2-adic identification that was only sketched — so if you quoted 0.9025 to him before, the $1-\varepsilon$ result is actually the more rigorous of the two.
